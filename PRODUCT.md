## Users

## Product Purpose
Wil Sensing finds the jobs a business does by hand that are worth handing off, measures each one, builds them cheap-first, and governs them. The first map is a starting list. New jobs show up during and after the build. Success for the site: a visitor sees the problem, understands the four steps, and books a free 30-minute call.

## Positioning
"Hand off the busywork. Keep the decisions." A third line on speed is undecided and is not on the site. No timeline promise. Reliability is the edge. Internal line: "the layer that makes AI reliable" (not stated as an absolute on the site). Public wording: AI only where it helps; plain automation where the work has to be exact. Lightweight and fast: no six-month study, no slide deck.

## Operating Context
Fit call (free, 30 minutes, no commitment; can end in a no) → Job map (free, short; every applicable job, each with a measure and a baseline where possible; the owner keeps it) → Build (the first paid step; price sized to the jobs on the map, no amount published; retire, then plain automation, then AI only for messy input; tested before trust) → Run and govern (monthly, stoppable, living job-list review). No prices are published. There is no map fee and no fee credit.

## Design Standard: less is more
Source: Jakub Krehel, "Less is more, more or less" (https://jakub.kr/writing/less-is-more). Wil adopted it 2026-10-24.
- Before adding any element, line of copy, motion, or code, say why it's there and whether the page gets worse without it. If you can't say, cut it.
- One message once. If two elements say the same thing, keep the one with proof.
- One primary action per screen. Contact lives in the closing section only.
- No decorative motion. Nothing rotates or animates on its own. Hover states change instantly.
- One list pattern (rows) across the site; no cards-as-structure, no filler labels.
- Prefer the smallest change that fully solves the problem. Stop when nothing else can be removed without loss.
- Review step: list every element of a draft, remove each in your head, keep only what the result gets worse without.

## Product Principles
1. The visitor's job comes first; the method comes second.
2. Every claim is real and labeled honestly.
3. Speak to everyone with a repeat job; exclude no one.
4. Show reliability through evidence, not adjectives.
5. One clear next step: the 30-minute call.
