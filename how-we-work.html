Four steps. The first two are free.

We find the jobs worth handing off, however many apply. Each one gets a measure and a baseline, and the work is sized to that set.

The four steps

  1. 1

    Fit call

    Free. 30 minutes. No commitment. We talk about how the work runs and where the hand work piles up. It ends in a Job map, a no and why, or not yet.

  2. 2

    Job map

    Free, and short. No six-month study and no slide deck. We try to find every job worth handing off. There is no set number.

    Improve [measure] from [baseline] to [target] by [date].

    If we can't write that sentence for a job, it stays off the build. You keep the map: the jobs, the measures and baselines, what the system does and what a person still does, the order to build them, and a price and timeline for the build. It's yours whether or not you go ahead. The first list is never the last.

  3. 3

    Build

    The first paid step. The price is sized to the jobs on the map. Retire what isn't needed. Use plain automation where the work has to be exact. Use AI only for messy input, like emails, notes, and photos. It's tested on real work before it's trusted. It starts by drafting for your OK and earns more freedom by passing tests. When something new shows up mid-build, we write it down and do one of three things: fix it inside the job, agree a written change with you before going on, or add it to the list for later. The build stays on what was scoped.

  4. 4

    Run and check, monthly

    Monthly, and you can stop. We watch each job against its measure, report it, and review the list with you: what's new, what changed, and what's next. A job that keeps passing can take on more. New jobs from the list get a short map of their own.

  • The rules hold.

    No rules get bent.

  • It can be measured.

    We can write the measure sentence for it.
