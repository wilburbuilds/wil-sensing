The biggest AI workflows, ripe for agent-ification in each department, that we've seen at Varick:

1. Finance
a. Manage AP exceptions: most AP teams are managing 600-800 exceptions a month where invoices don't match POs or receipts and require manual resolution
b. Automate your month end close: background execution of bank recs, accruals, intercompany, etc. can get your close cycle from 18-22 days to 7-9 days
c. Cash application and collections: automate matching remittance advice to open invoices and getting customers to pay on time

2. Sales Ops
a. Manage CRM data and rep admin: we see sales reps spend 38% of their time updating Salesforce
b. Deal desk approvals: we see deals take 23 days to get discounts, pricing, etc. approved
c. Prepare for forecast calls: ensure all the right data is available for each deal to prepare for Monday forecast calls (call notes, stage changes, next steps, etc.)

3. HR
a. Manage payroll changes: compare your ATS, HRIS, and payroll systems before payroll to make sure all new hires, raises, terminations, etc. are accurate. Most payroll corrections are due to issues before the payroll run.
b. Handle employee inquiries: we've seen up to 80% of inquiries for PTO, benefits, policy questions, etc. can be handled without human intervention
c. Onboarding: manage the process from offer acceptance to day 1, including background checks, equipment, account provisioning, I-9, etc.

4. Legal
a. Redline contracts: reduce turnaround time from 5+ days to same day for first pass redlines on NDAs, MSAs, vendor contracts, etc. based on your contract playbook
b. Contract intake: manage the intake process for contracts, including determining the requestor, type of contract, contract template, contract lawyer, etc.
c. Track contract obligations: extract contract renewal dates, notice periods, auto-renewal provisions, etc. from signed contracts

5. Operations
a. Disruption management: move from a week-long process to manage shipment delays, supplier issues, etc. to a few hours
b. Order management exceptions: many orders have exceptions that require manual management, whether it's a customer on credit hold, bad ship address, lack of inventory, etc.

6. Procurement
a. Supplier onboarding: reduce the time it takes to onboard new suppliers from 26 days to 4 by automating collection of W-9s, banking info, insurance, sanctions, etc.
b. 3-way match: compare invoices to POs and receiving documents to identify instances of duplicate invoices, invoices over PO price, etc.

7. IT
a. Joiners, movers, and leavers: ensure employees have all the right access on day 1, instead of day 4, and remove access the day they leave
b. Ticket routing: we see 1 in 3 IT tickets are routed to the wrong team and require rerouting

8. Marketing
a. Speed to lead: enrich, score, and route inbound demo requests to the right sales rep in under 5 minutes
b. Media reconciliation: reconcile media invoices to insertion orders and media actually run to identify the 8-12% of media spend that is lost

9. Customer Operations
a. Route escalations to the right team the first time, instead of having them bounce back and forth between support and engineering multiple times
b. Create case summaries to ensure your engineering team doesn't send cases back for additional details

All of these can be run in the background using your existing systems (NetSuite, Salesforce, SAP, ServiceNow, Dynamics, etc.) and exceptions can be routed to your people.

If you can solve these for your clients, you'll make them very happy.

Everyone says AI will kill litigation.
They have it backwards.

AI is about to make litigation explode.
For 100 years, American litigation has been limited by one thing. The hourly rate.

A breach of contract worth $40,000? The lawyer wants $25,000 to fight it. You settle for $12,000 and call it a win.

A customer stiffs you for $8,000? Not worth the demand letter. You eat it.

A former employee walks off with your client list? Your lawyer says "we can send something, but the motion practice alone will run you $30,000." You let it go.

That is how most American disputes end. Not in court. In a spreadsheet, where someone decides the fight costs more than the loss.

Now change one number.

A demand letter that billed 4 hours now takes 4 minutes.

A motion to dismiss that cost $15,000 in associate time now costs $15 in compute.

A 60 page discovery response that took a paralegal two weeks lands in your inbox by 7 a.m.

What happens to the spreadsheet?
Every "not worth it" flips to "why not."
Economists have a name for this. Jevons paradox.

In the 1860s, steam engines got more efficient. Everyone expected Britain to burn less coal. Britain burned more. 

Cheaper power meant more factories, more railways, more uses nobody had planned for.

Bandwidth got cheap. We didn't send fewer emails. We built Netflix.

Legal drafting gets cheap. People will not fight less. They will fight more.

And here's the twist nobody is talking about.

Not all legal work moves the same way.
Cooperative work shrinks. Both sides want the same standard NDA, AI hands it over in 60 seconds, the deal closes. That market dies.

Adversarial work grows. The moment your opponent has AI, every filing they send forces a bigger reply from you. Every motion breeds a cross motion. There is no ceiling on an arms race.
Then it all hits a wall. 

AI can 10x the filings. It cannot 10x the judges. Federal courts already have cases waiting years for trial. State courts are worse.

So picture it. Ten times the motions. The same number of judges. Clerks drowning. Dockets that stretch into the next decade.
Something breaks. I don't know what. But it won't be boring.

If you're a young litigator, here's what that means for you.

The paperwork was never going to be your career. The machine has that now.
The growth is in the fight.

Learn to take a deposition. Learn to find the hole in a 60 page brief that a machine wrote in 10 minutes. Learn to stand in front of a judge with 300 cases on her docket and make yours the one she remembers.

The machine drafts.

Someone still has to win.

Make sure it's you.
