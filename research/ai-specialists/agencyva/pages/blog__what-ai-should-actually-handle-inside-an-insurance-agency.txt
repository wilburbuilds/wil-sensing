What AI Should Actually Handle Inside an Insurance Agency
Workforce & Leadership
Sep 01 · 9 min read

nsurance agencies have no shortage of software. Most already work across an AMS, carrier portals, email, phone systems, spreadsheets, reporting tools, and a collection of smaller applications added over time.

AI introduces another question: which parts of the work should actually be handed to it?

The useful answer is narrower than “anything repetitive.” Insurance work involves judgment, licensing requirements, customer relationships, incomplete information, and situations where context matters more than speed.

AI is most valuable when it helps a team process information, identify what deserves attention, and handle structured work at scale. It becomes less useful when it is expected to make decisions that require accountability, professional judgment, or a nuanced client conversation.

For agencies evaluating AI, the distinction matters.

Start with information-heavy work

A large part of agency operations involves finding, reviewing, and organizing information before anyone can take action.

That is a natural place for AI.

Consider a renewal approaching in the next few weeks. Before the account manager decides what to do, they may need to understand the client’s policies, premium, recent activity, previous conversations, open tasks, and any changes in the account.

The difficult part is not always the decision itself. It is assembling the context required to make it.

AI can reduce that work by helping teams surface information such as:

upcoming renewals that may require attention
clients with possible coverage gaps
old quotes that never converted
recent call activity and outcomes
outstanding tasks associated with an account
unusual or potentially duplicate client records

The person still decides what happens next. They simply begin with a clearer picture.

Let AI find patterns humans do not have time to look for

Most agencies have more information in their book than their teams can realistically examine account by account.

That creates blind spots.

A producer may know their largest accounts extremely well while having little visibility into hundreds of smaller relationships. A service team may recognize an obvious cross-sell opportunity during a conversation but never have enough time to review the rest of the book systematically.

AI can perform that broader review.

For example, it can help identify customers who hold one line of coverage but appear to be missing another relevant line. It can also help prioritize those opportunities so the team is not presented with an undifferentiated list of every possible match.

The same principle applies to retention. Instead of waiting for a renewal to become urgent, agencies can use AI to surface accounts that appear to deserve an earlier review.

This is where AI becomes particularly useful: not replacing the person who understands the client, but making sure that person knows where to look.

Use AI to reduce the administrative work around calls

Phone conversations produce a surprising amount of secondary work.

Someone has to document what happened, categorize the call, record the outcome, decide whether follow-up is required, and make that information available to whoever handles the account next.

AI-assisted calling systems can reduce some of that overhead.

A properly designed system can record details such as the call direction, duration, outcome, category, sentiment, summary, and transcript. Instead of relying entirely on handwritten notes or memory, the next person can quickly understand what happened.

There are also situations where an AI voice agent can handle a structured conversation itself.

The important word is structured.

Clear workflows with known objectives are much better candidates than conversations involving unusual circumstances, sensitive client concerns, or decisions requiring licensed expertise.

When the conversation moves beyond what the AI should handle, the workflow should make it easy to bring in a person.

The goal is not maximum automation

The most automated agency is not automatically the best-run agency.

Some work benefits enormously from automation. Some benefits from better information. Some should remain entirely human.

The practical goal is to decide which is which.

Use AI where it can review more information than a person realistically could, reduce repetitive administrative work, organize activity, and help teams identify what deserves attention.

Keep people responsible for the work that depends on judgment, trust, licensing, accountability, and understanding the individual client.

That division of responsibility is less dramatic than the promise that AI will “run your agency.”

It is also considerably more useful.
