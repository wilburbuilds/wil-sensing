5.2 The Discovery Call: Structure, Script, and Flow

This is your most important call.

It determines:

Whether the prospect is a fit
Whether you can deliver results
Whether the project will be profitable
Whether you’re dealing with chaos or clarity

Here’s the exact discovery call structure:

Step 1: Frame the Call (2 minutes)

Set expectations and take control without sounding controlling.

Script:

“Great to meet you. Here’s how I’d like to run this call.

First, I’m going to get context on your business and your workflows.

Then we’ll break down the areas where automation and AI can create leverage.

If there’s a fit, I’ll outline what the path forward looks like. Sound good?”

This eliminates surprises and positions you as the expert.

Step 2: Business Overview (3–5 minutes)

Ask high-level questions:

“Tell me about the business in 60 seconds?”
“Who do you serve?”
“What volume do you handle monthly?”
“Which teams rely most on manual work?”
“What tools do you use today?”

You’re not looking for detail here. You’re building context.

Step 3: Workflow Deep-Dive (10–15 minutes)

This is where most agencies fail.

You must be better.

Break down their operations step by step:

Ask these questions in order:

1. “Walk me through your process from start to finish.”

Let them talk.

Don’t interrupt.

2. “What parts of this process are the most manual?”

3. “Where do things get stuck or delayed?”

4. “Where does information get duplicated?”

5. “Which tasks require the most human time today?”

6. “Which parts produce the most errors?”

7. “What systems or tools are disconnected?”

8. “Who is responsible for what?”

9. “What parts could break if volume increased?”

10. “What is the cost of these bottlenecks?”

This sequence uncovers everything you need.

Your job is to identify:

Data re-entry
Disconnected systems
Manual approvals
Human bottlenecks
Siloed tools
Excel dependence
Email dependence
Communication gaps
Broken handoffs

These are your leverage points.

Step 4: Pain Quantification (5 minutes)

Most prospects underestimate their bottleneck cost.

Your job is to put real numbers behind the chaos.

Ask these:

“How many hours per week are spent on this?”
“What’s the hourly cost of the people doing it?”
“What errors or delays come from this?”
“What’s the impact on throughput or revenue?”
“If volume doubled, what would break first?”

This creates urgency without you selling anything.

Step 5: Identify the High-Leverage Opportunities (5 minutes)

Here’s where you shift from listening to diagnosing.

Summarize:

“Here’s what I’m seeing so far:

Two major bottlenecks
Three repetitive processes
No central source of truth
Tools that aren’t integrated
High manual work around X and Y
A lot of avoidable rework
Clear opportunities for systemization”

Then:

Present a brief vision of the system you’d build.

Not tools.

Not features.

Systems.

Examples:

“You need an intake-to-delivery operating system.”
“Your sales workflow needs a single source of truth.”
“Your onboarding process needs automation at every handoff.”
“You need an AI agent acting as the first layer of decision-making.”
“Your data needs to live in one place with automation layered on top.”

This is where authority is created.

Step 6: Present the 3-Pillar Strategy Process (3 minutes)

The client should not be deciding whether to “buy automation.”

They should be deciding whether to get clarity.

Here’s the exact scripting:

Pillar 1: Mapping & Diagnostics

“We map every workflow, touchpoint, and bottleneck in your business so we know exactly where automation creates the highest ROI.”

Pillar 2: Architecture & Recommendations

“We design the optimized workflows, the AI agents, the automations, and the system architecture.”

Pillar 3: Implementation Plan

“You receive a full build plan, timelines, cost estimates, and ROI calculations.”

This process reframes:

“We’re not guessing.”
“We’re not implementing blindly.”
“We’re building with precision.”

This alone differentiates you from 99% of agencies.

Step 7: Next Steps & Close (3 minutes)

Here’s the exact closing script:

“Based on everything we covered today, the next step is the Strategy Process.

It takes 10–14 days.

The investment is $2,000.

Half of that applies to implementation if we move forward.

Would you like to secure a spot?”

You’re not pitching.

You’re presenting what happens next.

5.3 The Qualification Framework: Who You Should Work With

Not all clients are worth it.

Use the SCORE method to qualify:

S: Severity of the Problems

Are they losing time, money, capacity, or accuracy at scale?

C: Complexity of the Workflows

The more complex, the more valuable your involvement.

O: Operational Commitment

Do they actually want to improve their systems?

R: Resources

Budget, team, tech environment.

E: Expansion Potential

Is this a one-off, or multi-department opportunity?

If the SCORE is low, you walk away.
