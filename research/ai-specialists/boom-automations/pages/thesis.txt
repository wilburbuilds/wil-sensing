Our thesis

Nothing matters more than adoption.

A system nobody uses is a cost, not a result. Everything about how Boom works comes from that, and from a few beliefs we have held since the first automation we ever built.

Where we started

The tools changed. The process didn't.

Boom started almost five years ago with basic automations: Zapier, Airtable, the simple glue between tools a team already had. The tools have changed completely since then. The process has not. The value was never the tool. It was sitting with the team, understanding how the work actually moves, and building the thing that removed the most friction. That consultative approach is still the whole business.

Today the systems are AI operating systems with agents running whole workflows. The way we get there is the same as it was with a Zapier zap: assess first, build with the team, train them to own it.

The process we run

Four steps, every engagement, since the beginning.

Each step exists to protect adoption and ROI. None of them are optional.

01
Current state assessment

Adoption starts with picking the right problem. We sit with the people who do the work and map how the department actually runs: where the hours go, where the cost sits, which tools exist only because nothing better did. That is where the biggest opportunities are, including the SaaS that can come out of the budget entirely.

02
Solution design

The operating system gets designed around what moves the numbers, not around what is easiest to automate. We decide what to keep, what to consolidate, what to automate cheaply, and where agents belong.

03
Build with the team

An agile cadence that works for your team. Short live demos where they watch each piece come together, and feedback sessions where they tell us what to change while it still costs nothing to change. Designed to take load off a busy team, not add to it.

04
Enablement and ongoing management

We train your team to run, audit, and extend the system, with enablement training updated every quarter and access for life. After launch we keep measuring and improving it. Managed, not handed off.

What we believe

Four things we have never changed our mind on.
01
Adoption and ROI are the only numbers that matter.

A system the team does not run is a cost. A system that runs but does not move a number the CFO cares about is a hobby. We measure both from the first week and design the whole engagement around protecting them.

Across our deployments, adoption sits at 94%. It is not a side effect. It is the thing everything else is built to produce.

02
One-off AI tools were never the answer.

We never believed in vertical AI SaaS: a different agent for every task, a different subscription for every department, none of them sharing context. It fragments the work instead of fixing it, and it leaves your team as the glue again.

One operating system with a constant context layer is the alternative. Start with one department, add the next on the same system, and the whole business ends up with full visibility instead of twelve dashboards.

03
Enablement, not replacement.

We do not build systems to remove people. We build them so the people you have can do more, and we train them to run what we built. Growth for the team is the point.

That is why every engagement includes AI enablement training, updated every quarter as the tools change, with access for life. The capability has to live inside your team, not inside your vendor.

04
The team has to build it with us.

Change management campaigns exist because teams get handed systems they never saw being built. We skip the campaign by skipping the handoff: your people are in the room from the first demo to the first rollout.

When the first piece goes live, there is no hesitation. They watched it get built. They had a hand in it.
