What Is a Fractional COO?

A fractional COO is a part-time operations executive who builds and runs the systems a growing business needs, without the cost of a full-time hire. For a founder-led service company, that means assigning clear ownership of every function, setting up a weekly scorecard and meeting rhythm, documenting how the work gets done, and moving day-to-day decisions off the owner’s plate.

What does a fractional COO actually do?

A fractional COO installs the operating system the business has outgrown not having. In practice that work includes:

Defining who owns each function, outcome and decision, so work stops falling through the cracks.
Setting up a weekly scorecard with the few numbers that show whether the business is on track.
Running a weekly operating meeting that ends in decisions and clear to-dos.
Documenting the core processes so the work gets done the same way every time.
Coaching managers until they can make the calls the owner used to make.
When does a service business need a fractional COO?

A service business usually needs a fractional COO when growth has outrun its systems and too much still runs through the owner. Common signs:

Your team waits on you for approvals they could make themselves.
The same problems come back every week.
The office and the field each think the other owns it.
You can’t see where the money actually goes.
A week off means the phone never stops.
How is a fractional COO different from a consultant, a coach or a full-time COO?

A consultant typically recommends, a coach typically advises, and a fractional COO installs the system and stays until the team runs it. A full-time COO is a permanent executive hire; a fractional COO does the same kind of work part-time, which fits companies that need the system before they can justify the salary.
