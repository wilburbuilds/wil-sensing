IT Services for Dealer Groups Running More Than One Rooftop

One security, network and support standard at every rooftop, including the next store you buy.

Get Your Complimentary IT & Cybersecurity Assessment →

Last updated: October 8, 2026

IT services for dealer groups put every rooftop on one standard for security, networks, user accounts and support, run by one provider. Helion Technologies does this for automotive and heavy truck groups with stores across multiple states.

Why Dealer Group IT Gets Harder With Every Rooftop

Most groups don’t design their IT. They inherit it. The first store runs the firewall somebody installed years ago, and the third came with its own Microsoft 365 tenant, its own backup vendor and a shared login the F&I office still uses. Our map of the eight systems in a dealership technology stack shows who usually owns each one. Before the next budget cycle, our IT budget checklist for dealer groups turns that inherited mix into line items you can price.

Each store works, more or less. Together they’re hard to secure, because an attacker only needs the weakest rooftop to reach the email, DMS connections and accounting systems the whole group shares, and the weakest rooftop is usually the one nobody at the group office has looked at in a year.

The data at stake is huge. According to NADA Data 2025, the nation’s 16,990 franchised light-vehicle dealers sold 16.2 million vehicles and wrote more than 276 million repair orders last year. Every deal jacket, credit application and repair order in those stores holds names, addresses and often Social Security numbers that somebody inside the group, or a provider working for it, has to protect.

That’s the work IT services for dealer groups exist to do. It’s one part of the dealership IT and cybersecurity Helion Technologies has provided since 1997, only to automotive and heavy truck stores.

One IT Standard for Every Store in the Group

Standardizing is the most useful thing a dealer group can do for its IT. It’s also the least exciting. Helion advises groups to run the same hardware, software and security policies at every location, because consistent systems are simpler to support and harder to break into.

Here’s what we line up across rooftops.

User accounts and Microsoft 365, so a salesperson who moves between stores keeps one login and loses all access the day they leave.
Firewalls, switches and Wi-Fi built to one design. Fix a problem at one store and you’ve fixed it everywhere.
Endpoint protection and patching on a single schedule.
Backups tested on the same calendar, because a backup nobody has restored is only a hope.
DMS roles and access, whether the group runs CDK, Reynolds and Reynolds or Tekion, with our DMS services team behind it.
Group-Wide Cybersecurity and Compliance

One store’s breach becomes the group’s problem fast. A phished F&I manager at one rooftop can open the door to shared email and accounting, which is why Helion’s 24/7 security monitoring watches every store in the group from the same security operations center.

Compliance runs the same way. The FTC Safeguards Rule covers dealerships that arrange financing or leasing, and it expects risk assessments, testing and a written report to the board or owners at least once a year. One compliance program across the group beats rebuilding the paperwork store by store.

Automakers are pushing too. Mercedes-Benz USA now requires dealers to prove an effective security program through ISO/IEC 27001 or TISAX Level 2 certification by September 30, 2026, part of a wider shift we cover in why automakers are raising the dealership cybersecurity bar.

Comparing providers? Our buyer’s guide for multi-rooftop groups lists the questions worth asking.

When the Group Buys Another Store

Acquisitions are where group IT usually breaks. A new rooftop can bring incompatible systems, inconsistent security policies and user access nobody has audited, and the clock starts at closing. Our page on dealership acquisition IT integration walks through each phase of a buy-sell.

1
Look before you close

We review the target store’s network, DMS setup, Microsoft 365 and security gaps, so the surprises show up in diligence instead of on day one.

2
Plan day-one access

Email, user accounts and the systems staff need on their first morning under the group’s name get planned with your team ahead of time.

3
Bring it to the group standard

The store moves onto the group’s network design, endpoint protection, backups and policies on a schedule that doesn’t stall the sales floor.

4
Same support line

Staff at the new rooftop call the same number as everyone else in the group. If the deal includes a DMS switch, our DMS conversion team plans it alongside the rest.
