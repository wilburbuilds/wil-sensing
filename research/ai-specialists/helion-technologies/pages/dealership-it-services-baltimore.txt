Maryland Privacy and Security Rules for Dealerships

Two governments have a say in how a Baltimore dealership protects customer data. Washington wrote the FTC Safeguards Rule. Annapolis wrote two more laws, and they don’t treat a dealership the same way. Here’s the short version. It isn’t legal advice, so run the details past your attorney.

Sources are the FTC’s Safeguards Rule guide, 16 CFR Part 314 and the Maryland General Assembly’s record of the 2024 privacy act.

RULE	WHAT IT COVERS	IN FORCE	AT YOUR STORE
FTC Safeguards Rule	
A written security program, a named person in charge, risk assessments, multifactor authentication, encryption and testing
Applies to dealers	June 2023	The baseline for every store that arranges financing or leases. The nine requirements
FTC breach notice	
Tell the FTC within 30 days when unencrypted information on 500 or more customers is taken
Applies to dealers	May 2024	What dealers must report
Maryland Online Data Privacy Act	
Consumer data rights and limits on collection for businesses holding data on 35,000 or more Maryland consumers
Dealership exempt under GLBA	October 1, 2025	Covers data handled from April 1, 2026 forward
Maryland Personal Information Protection Act	
Reasonable security for personal information, plus notice to affected residents within 45 days of discovering a breach
Applies, GLBA compliance counts	Amended 2022	The Attorney General hears about it before your customers do
Exempt Doesn’t Mean Off the Hook

Maryland’s privacy law took effect on October 1, 2025. It exempts financial institutions covered by the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act, and that’s the category most franchised dealers land in once they arrange financing. Good news. Sort of.

The exemption moves the work. It doesn’t remove it. We wrote about why Maryland’s privacy law matters to dealers before it took effect, and the advice hasn’t changed much since, which is to build one security program strong enough that you never have to care which rulebook an examiner, a lender or an automaker happens to open first.

That’s the job of our dealership cybersecurity team and the FTC Safeguards compliance program. Want a starting point this week? Print the FTC Safeguards Rule checklist. To follow what Annapolis does next, watch the Maryland Automobile Dealers Association, which speaks for about 300 dealership rooftops across the state.
