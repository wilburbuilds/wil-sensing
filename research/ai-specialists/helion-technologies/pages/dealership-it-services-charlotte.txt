Dealership IT Services in Charlotte Cross a State Line

Drive 20 minutes south on I-77 from uptown and you’re in South Carolina. Most people here don’t even notice the sign anymore. A group with a store in Concord, another in Matthews and a third in Rock Hill feels like one local company, and it is, but its customer data sits under two different state breach laws, and its stores report to two different consumer protection offices when something goes wrong.

That’s the part of this market people miss. The IT has to be one system. The paperwork has to answer to two states.

What June 2024 Looked Like From Charlotte

On June 19, 2024, CDK Global told its dealers it had shut down systems after a cyberattack. Sonic Automotive, the public dealer group headquartered right here in Charlotte, reported two days later in an 8-K filed with the SEC that its dealer management system and its CRM were down, and that every one of its stores stayed open on workaround processes. By its second-quarter earnings release, the estimate was in. About $30.0 million off pre-tax income, or $0.64 a share. That was one quarter.

Nobody at those stores did anything wrong. The outage came in through a vendor, and that’s the lesson worth keeping. A store’s IT plan has to cover the day a system it doesn’t own goes dark, which means printed deal forms, a way to look up a customer without the DMS, a phone tree that works when email doesn’t and backups of your own data that a vendor’s trouble can’t touch. Plan for the outage. Start with our DMS outage plan, then see how backup and disaster recovery for dealerships fits around it, and keep the incident response plan template where the managers can find it.

We wrote at the time that CDK wasn’t a one-time wake-up call, and we still think so. Running several rooftops around Charlotte? The dealer groups page shows how one team covers all of them. Buying another store? Our page on dealership acquisition IT integration covers what to check before closing.

North Carolina and South Carolina Rules for Charlotte Dealerships

Four lines matter here. One federal rule does most of the work. Each state has its own breach law, and they’re close but not the same. The fourth line is the one people ask about most, and right now it’s empty.

RULE	WHAT IT IS	DATE OR DEADLINE	AT THE STORE
FTC Safeguards Rule	
Federal rule for dealers that arrange financing or leasing
Applies	Amended rule in force June 9, 2023	A written security program, a qualified individual, multi-factor authentication, encryption, testing and vendor oversight. Get the checklist
North Carolina Identity Theft Protection Act	
N.C. General Statutes, Section 75-65
Applies	Without unreasonable delay	Every notice to customers also goes to the Attorney General’s Consumer Protection Division. Above 1,000 people, the credit bureaus are told too.
South Carolina breach law	
S.C. Code, Section 39-1-90
Applies	Most expedient time possible	Above 1,000 people, notify the Department of Consumer Affairs and the credit bureaus as well as the customers.
Comprehensive privacy law	
A Virginia-style consumer privacy act
Not enacted	None in either state as of October 2026	North Carolina lawmakers have filed bills. None has passed, so nothing extra applies today.

Start with the breach laws, because both apply to any business holding residents’ personal information. North Carolina’s Section 75-65 wants customers told without unreasonable delay, and every time a business sends those notices, it also has to tell the state’s Consumer Protection Division what happened and how many people were affected. South Carolina’s Section 39-1-90 says the most expedient time possible, and it only pulls in the state’s Department of Consumer Affairs once more than 1,000 residents are involved. Close, not identical.

A group with stores in Charlotte and Fort Mill could owe both on the same morning, plus the FTC. The federal rule in 16 CFR 314.4 needs a written security program and its own 30-day notice to the FTC once 500 or more consumers are involved. Your attorney decides how each rule applies to you, and our dealership compliance team keeps the program that answers all three. Stores in other states too? Compare them in dealership privacy laws by state, and follow rule changes as they happen in FTC Safeguards Rule news.

Dealership Cybersecurity in Charlotte

Ask a Charlotte general manager how many outside companies can reach into the store’s network and you’ll usually get a guess. The real list runs longer than anyone expects. There’s the DMS, the CRM, the phone system, the OEM portals, the digital retailing tool, the F&I menu and whoever installed the cameras, and each one of those connections is a door somebody has to watch. Few stores have ever counted.

The numbers back that up. CDK Global’s 2024 survey of dealers found that 35% had a cyberattack or incident that year, and 92% of them said it did real financial or operational damage. Our dealership cybersecurity work covers the store’s side of it. A live, US-based security operations center watches the network day and night through managed detection and response, advanced endpoint detection and response guards the computers, and multi-factor authentication goes on email and remote access for everyone who uses them. Microsoft 365 security closes the inbox where most attacks start, the basics on preventing phishing at a dealership still hold up, security awareness training teaches everyone with a login to spot the fakes, and the state of dealership cybersecurity shows the bigger picture.
