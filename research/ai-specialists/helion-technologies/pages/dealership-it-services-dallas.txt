Three Rules a Dallas Dealership Answers To

Texas keeps this simpler than some states do. One federal rule carries most of the weight, the state privacy law mostly steps aside for dealers that arrange financing, and the state breach law applies to anybody holding a customer file. Three rules. Here’s the short version.

RULE	WHAT IT IS	DATE OR DEADLINE	AT THE STORE
FTC Safeguards Rule	
Federal rule for dealers that arrange financing or leasing
Applies	Amended rule in force June 9, 2023	A written security program, a qualified individual, multi-factor authentication, encryption, testing and vendor oversight.
Texas Data Privacy and Security Act	
Texas’s comprehensive consumer privacy law
Dealership exempt	In force July 1, 2024	Financial institutions under the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act are exempt as whole entities.
Texas breach notification law	
Business and Commerce Code, Section 521.053
Applies	60 days to notify people	Tell the Texas attorney general within 30 days when 250 or more Texans are affected.

Why does a dealership get the entity-level pass? A store that arranges financing or leases vehicles is treated as a financial institution under the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act, and the Texas statute exempts those outright. That’s the common reading. Ask your attorney how it fits your stores, because a group can own businesses that don’t finance anything. We cover how to check each one in what the Texas privacy law means for dealer groups.

None of that shrinks the federal job. The Safeguards Rule in 16 CFR 314.4 still expects a written security program, plus a notice to the FTC within 30 days when a breach touches 500 or more consumers, and Texas runs its own clock on top of that through the attorney general’s data breach reporting process. Two clocks. Our dealership compliance team keeps both in one program.

Dealership Cybersecurity in Dallas, TX

June 2024 is the month every dealer remembers. When CDK Global went down, Penske Automotive told investors in an SEC filing that the outage reached Premier Truck Group, its Dallas-based commercial truck business with 48 locations, which kept running on manual and alternate processes. Paper and patience. We said it then and still mean it. CDK wasn’t a one-time wake-up call.

Nobody at a store can stop a vendor outage. What a store can control is its own side of the wire, and that’s where our dealership cybersecurity work lives. A live, US-based security operations center watches the network day and night. Multi-factor authentication goes on email and remote access. Backups get a test restore, because a backup nobody has ever restored is only a hope.
