Dealership IT Services in Denver Run the Length of I-25

A Denver dealer group can have a store on Havana Street in Aurora, another off Arapahoe Road near I-25 in Centennial and a third two hours up I-70 in a ski town, and only two of those are easy to reach in February. The DMS, the phones and the logins are supposed to work the same way at all three. Often they don’t.

Helion Technologies already supports client dealerships in the Denver metro, and what we find here looks a lot like what we fix everywhere else. Stores bought one at a time. Each one came with its own vendor, its own firewall and its own workaround for the last outage, and nobody ever had the time to line them all up.

That’s the work we like. Helion Technologies has supported automotive and heavy truck dealerships, and nobody else, since 1997. This page is part of our dealership IT services by location hub, and it covers what’s different about running stores around Denver.

Watched around the clock
Security support runs 24/7, with live, US-based analysts who step in the moment they see signs of an attack.

Our dealership help desk keeps dealer hours. Whenever your Denver stores are open, 7 days a week, the help desk is open too. Service drive in Lakewood opening early on a Saturday? We’re on. The last F&I deal in Thornton running late? Still here. We close for Thanksgiving and Christmas and that’s it. Two days a year. After-hours emergencies, weekends and field support come at no extra charge inside the one monthly number, so the bill doesn’t jump because a problem turned out bigger than it looked. Ask any provider for the help desk metrics a dealer principal should see, get them written into a dealership IT support SLA, and compare.

One Standard From Fort Collins to the Mountain Stores

Denver groups rarely stop at the metro line. There’s a store in Fort Collins, one in Colorado Springs, maybe one up in the mountains that sells a lot of four-wheel drives to people who need them by November, and every one of them is a long drive from whoever fixes things. When the internet line drops at the mountain store, the DMS, the phones and the card readers go with it, and that’s the store that can least afford to wait for someone to come up the pass. Snow doesn’t wait.

So we plan for it. Every store gets the same network, the same security and the same help desk, and our dealership network services add a second internet line that takes over on its own when the first one fails. Remote tools fix most problems. Not all of them. When a store needs hands, a field technician goes, and the trip never comes back as its own invoice.

A 12-location heavy truck group we support in the Southeast had the same trouble with outages at its rural stores. After we rebuilt its hardware and network, its COO said, “they’re getting the phone answered or getting a call back within five minutes.” Five minutes. That story and others are in Helion’s dealership case studies, dealers describe working with us in their own words on our client testimonials page, and our dealer groups page covers IT across many rooftops. Onboarding starts within weeks of signing.

Colorado’s Rules for a Dealership’s Data

Colorado sits in an odd spot. Its big privacy act leaves most dealerships out, an older security law that gets far less attention still applies, and a new law on automated decisions is on the way. Six lines matter here.

RULE	WHAT IT IS	DATE OR DEADLINE	AT THE STORE
FTC Safeguards Rule	
Federal rule for dealers that arrange financing or leasing
Applies	Amended rule in force June 9, 2023	A written security program, a qualified individual, multi-factor authentication, encryption and testing, plus notice to the FTC within 30 days when 500 or more consumers are involved. Get the checklist
Colorado security law	
C.R.S. 6-1-713.5
Applies	In force since September 1, 2018	Reasonable security for customer data that fits the size of the store, and vendor contracts that make your vendors protect it too.
Colorado disposal law	
C.R.S. 6-1-713
Applies	Written policy required since September 1, 2018	A written policy for destroying paper and electronic records with personal data once you no longer need them, old deal jackets and retired hard drives included.
Colorado breach law	
C.R.S. 6-1-716
Applies	30 days from determining a breach happened	Affected Coloradans told inside 30 days, the Attorney General too when 500 or more are affected, and the credit bureaus above 1,000. An incident response plan keeps the count ready.
Colorado Privacy Act	
C.R.S. 6-1-1301 and following
Dealers usually exempt	In force July 1, 2023	Exempts a financial institution subject to the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act and its affiliates, which takes most franchised dealers out of it.
Colorado automated decision law	
SB 26-189, which replaced the 2024 AI Act
Ask your attorney	Requirements start January 1, 2027	Notice before software that materially influences a financing decision is used, a path to human review after a bad outcome, and 3 years of records.

The security and breach laws are the ones that catch Denver stores off guard. They apply whether or not the privacy act covers you, and the 30-day clock starts when you work out that a breach happened, not when the investigation wraps up. Thirty days goes fast. Faster still if nobody kept the logs. Our blueprint for Colorado auto and truck dealers walks through both laws, and a written security program with a tested notice process covers most of what they ask for.

Colorado rewrote its AI law this year too. The 2024 Colorado AI Act was repealed in May 2026 and replaced by SB 26-189, which centers on automated decision-making technology and starts on January 1, 2027, so a store whose software helps decide who gets financing should ask its attorney now how it applies. Your attorney decides how each rule applies to you, and our dealership compliance team keeps one written program that answers to all of them. Dealership Cybersecurity in Denver

Most attacks on a dealership start with a person, not a server. A phishing email to the office manager. Or a call to the help desk from someone who says they work at your store and needs a password reset right now. That second one is why Helion has verified the identity of every person who calls our help desk for support since November 17, 2023, with a one-time code sent to the email or mobile number already on file, never to whatever number the caller offers.

It gets tested. In one real case, a vendor approved for a dealership’s corporate accounting let several of its own employees share a single login, and two of them called us posing as dealership staff. Both were refused. We told the dealership and handed over the call recordings, and the dealership replaced the vendor.

CDK Global’s 2024 survey of dealers found that 35% had a cyberattack or incident that year, and 92% of them said it did real financial or operational damage. That’s a lot of stores. Our dealership cybersecurity work covers the rest of the store. Managed detection and response keeps live analysts on the network around the clock, advanced endpoint detection and response guards the computers, and Microsoft 365 security closes the inbox where most attacks start.

Security awareness training built for dealership staff teaches people to spot the phishing email, and penetration testing shows where an attacker would get in. Want to know where your logins, backups and vendor access stand? Start with a complimentary IT and cybersecurity assessment. We’ll write the gaps down.
