When the Breach Is at Your Vendor

700Credit runs credit and identity checks for dealers from its offices in Southfield, a few minutes up the Lodge from downtown. On October 25, 2025 it found that someone had been pulling consumer records it had collected from dealers since May, getting in through a compromised integration partner. Names, addresses, birth dates and Social Security numbers. All of it. Michigan’s Attorney General put it at nearly 6 million people, more than 160,000 of them Michiganders.

The dealers whose customers were in those records hadn’t been hacked. Their customers got letters anyway, because it was their customers’ data, collected at their F&I desks, and 700Credit mailed those letters on the dealers’ behalf and filed one consolidated notice with the FTC for them. That’s about as good as a vendor breach gets for a store. Plenty of vendors won’t do any of it for you. Ask yours now.

The FTC Safeguards Rule makes watching your service providers part of your own program, and our dealership compliance team keeps a risk rating on every vendor that touches customer data. Our guide to evaluating dealership cybersecurity vendors covers the questions to ask before you sign. More stories are in Helion’s dealership case studies. Your vendor’s breach is still your customers’ breach.

Your Vendor’s Breach, Your Notice

Say a company that holds your customers’ credit applications gets broken into. Michigan law and the federal rule a dealership answers to each say who tells whom, and the order matters more than most stores think. Here’s the short version.

RULE	WHO TELLS WHOM	DEADLINE	AT THE STORE
Michigan breach law, MCL 445.72(2)	
The vendor tells the dealership, the business that owns the data
Vendor first	After the vendor discovers the breach, with no set number of days	A contract clause that says how fast a vendor has to tell you, and who at the store takes that call.
Michigan breach law, MCL 445.72(1)	
The dealership tells each affected Michigan resident
No day count	Without unreasonable delay, with time allowed to find the scope	Logs kept long enough to show whose records were taken, so letters go out to the right people.
Michigan breach law, MCL 445.72(8)	
The nationwide credit reporting agencies, after the residents
Over 1,000	When more than 1,000 Michigan residents get notices	Doesn’t apply to a business subject to the federal Gramm-Leach-Bliley privacy rules, so ask your attorney which side your store is on.
FTC Safeguards Rule, 16 CFR 314.4	
The dealership tells the FTC, when 500 or more consumers are involved
Federal	30 days from discovery	A written security program that makes vendors keep safeguards in place by contract and gets them reassessed.

Michigan doesn’t have a comprehensive consumer privacy law, so for a dealership here the state’s breach notice law and the federal rule do most of the work. Neither one cares much whose server got broken into. Under the FTC Safeguards Rule you have to pick vendors that can protect the data, hold them to it by contract and check on them from time to time, and the 30-day FTC notice covers your customers’ records wherever they sit. Thirty days goes quickly when the vendor takes two weeks to call. It happens. Read what dealers must report within 30 days.

Your attorney decides how each rule applies to you, and our dealership compliance team keeps one written program that answers to both. Start with a Safeguards risk assessment template, keep an incident response plan template filled in, or compare states in dealership privacy laws by state.

Dealership Cybersecurity in Detroit

In this town some of the security rules come straight from the factory. GM publishes dealer infrastructure and security guidelines for its stores, and our guide to OEM cybersecurity requirements covers the others dealers ask us about. Then there’s the outage nobody plans for. When the CDK Global outage hit in June 2024, Premier Truck Group, the heavy truck business of Bloomfield Hills-based Penske Automotive, kept its 48 locations open on manual and alternate processes, according to Penske’s own SEC filing. That only works if somebody wrote the plan down before the screens went dark. Write yours.

CDK Global’s 2024 survey of dealers found that 35% had a cyberattack or incident that year, and 92% of them said it did real financial or operational damage. Our dealership cybersecurity work covers the store’s side. A live, US-based security operations center watches the network through managed detection and response, advanced endpoint detection and response guards the computers, and Microsoft 365 security closes the inbox where most attacks start. Security awareness training teaches staff what a fake vendor call sounds like, and penetration testing shows where an attacker would get in.

Our DMS outage plan covers selling and servicing through a day without the DMS, and our help desk verifies every caller before it touches an account, so a voice claiming to be your vendor gets nowhere. Want to know where your logins, vendor access and backups stand? Start with a complimentary IT and cybersecurity assessment. We’ll write the gaps down.
