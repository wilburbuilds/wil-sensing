Three Rules a Houston Dealership Answers To

Good news first. Texas is simpler than a lot of states on this. One federal rule does most of the work, the state privacy law largely steps aside for a dealer that arranges financing, and the state breach law covers anyone who holds a customer file. Here’s the short version of all three.

RULE	WHAT IT IS	DATE OR DEADLINE	AT THE STORE
FTC Safeguards Rule	
Federal rule for dealers that arrange financing or leasing
Applies	Amended rule in force June 9, 2023	A written security program, a qualified individual, multi-factor authentication, encryption, testing and vendor oversight.
Texas Data Privacy and Security Act	
Texas’s comprehensive consumer privacy law
Dealership exempt	In force July 1, 2024	Financial institutions under the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act are exempt as whole entities.
Texas breach notification law	
Business and Commerce Code, Section 521.053
Applies	60 days to notify people	Tell the Texas attorney general within 30 days when 250 or more Texans are affected.

So why does a dealership get a pass on the state privacy law? A store that arranges financing or leases vehicles counts as a financial institution under the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act, and the Texas statute exempts those as whole entities. That’s the usual reading. Run it past your attorney anyway, because a group can own a rental counter or a body shop that doesn’t finance a thing. Our guide to the Texas Data Privacy and Security Act for car dealerships walks through which of those companies to check.

The federal job doesn’t get any smaller. The Safeguards Rule in 16 CFR 314.4 still expects a written security program and a notice to the FTC within 30 days when a breach touches 500 or more consumers, and Texas keeps its own deadline on top of that through the attorney general’s data breach reporting process. Two clocks, one incident. Our dealership compliance team runs both out of one program.

Auto Dealership Cybersecurity in Houston

Houston had a front-row seat in June 2024. When CDK Global went down, Group 1 Automotive, the public dealer group headquartered here, told investors in an SEC filing that it had activated its incident response procedures and isolated its systems from CDK’s platform, and that all of its U.S. dealerships kept doing business on alternative processes. That’s the right playbook. It’s also a lot of paper. As we wrote back then, CDK wasn’t a one-time wake-up call.

No store can prevent a vendor’s outage. What it can control is its own side of the connection, and that’s where our dealership cybersecurity work sits. A live, US-based security operations center watches the network day and night. Multi-factor authentication goes on email and remote access. And backups get a real test restore before hurricane season, because a backup nobody has ever restored is a guess, and the week a storm is sitting in the Gulf is a bad week to find that out.
