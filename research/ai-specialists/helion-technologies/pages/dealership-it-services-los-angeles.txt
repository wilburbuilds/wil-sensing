We Were Walking California Dealers Through the CCPA in 2019

California is where the privacy rules started, and we were there for the start. In April 2019, eight months before the California Consumer Privacy Act took effect, Helion partnered with a statewide association of franchised new car dealers to teach its members what the law would ask of them. What had to change on the network. What had to change in the handbook. And what “reasonable measures” would mean to a regulator reading a dealership’s file after something went wrong.

Nobody knew then how it would play out. We wrote the dealer’s guide to the CCPA that year, then a CCPA preparation checklist, and when voters passed the sequel in 2020 we covered the California Privacy Rights Act too. Seven years on, the law has grown teeth.

So a Los Angeles store isn’t our first California conversation. Not close. Running several rooftops? See how one team covers all of them on our dealer groups page.

Three Rules a Los Angeles Dealership Answers To

In most states a dealership gets a pass on the state privacy law. Not here. California exempts the financing paperwork and leaves the store itself on the hook, and since January 2026 its breach law runs on a fixed 30-day clock. Three rules, then, and all three apply.

RULE	WHAT IT IS	DATE OR DEADLINE	AT THE STORE
FTC Safeguards Rule	
Federal rule for dealers that arrange financing or leasing
Applies	Amended rule in force June 9, 2023	A written security program, a qualified individual, multi-factor authentication, encryption, testing and vendor oversight. Get the checklist
California Consumer Privacy Act	
The CCPA, as amended by the California Privacy Rights Act
Data-level exemption only	In force since January 1, 2020	Financing data held under the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act is carved out. Service, marketing, website and employee records are not.
California breach notification law	
Civil Code Section 1798.82
Applies	30 calendar days to notify people	Tell the attorney general within 15 days of that notice when more than 500 Californians are affected.

Why doesn’t the usual exemption work here? Texas, Virginia and most other states exempt a business that falls under the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act as a whole entity. The California Consumer Privacy Act exempts only the information collected under that federal law, so the credit application is carved out while the service history, the website lead, the marketing list and the personnel file all stay in. And the carve-out doesn’t reach the part of the law that lets customers sue after a breach. Our guide to dealership privacy laws by state shows how far California sits from the rest.

Two newer pieces are worth a date on the calendar. Senate Bill 446 put the 30-day notice deadline into the breach law on January 1, 2026, on top of the federal Safeguards Rule and its notice to the FTC within 30 days. And the state privacy agency’s new regulations add cybersecurity audits for businesses that meet its risk thresholds, with the first certifications due April 1, 2028. Does your group meet them? Ask your attorney. Our dealership compliance team builds one written security program that answers to all three.

Dealership Cybersecurity in Los Angeles, CA

You don’t have to look far for an example. The California attorney general keeps a public list of reported data breaches, and in May 2025 two Southern California stores under common ownership filed notices there for the same incident. Someone had been inside their network from August 21 to August 30, 2024. Nine days in. The notices reached the state more than eight months after that.

Under the law as it reads today, a gap like that would be hard to defend. Thirty days isn’t long when you still have to work out which files were touched and whose names were in them, and a store that has no logs to read can’t answer either question, which is exactly why our dealership cybersecurity work starts with visibility. A live, US-based security operations center watches the network day and night. Multi-factor authentication goes on email and remote access. Backups get a real test restore. The wider picture is in the state of dealership cybersecurity. It all matters more in a state where customers can sue over a breach, and as we wrote after the big DMS outage, CDK wasn’t a one-time wake-up call.
