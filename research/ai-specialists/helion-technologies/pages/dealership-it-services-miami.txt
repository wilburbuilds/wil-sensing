Hurricane Season Is Part of the IT Plan Here

Nobody in South Florida needs the weather explained. Hurricane Irma came through on September 10, 2017 and knocked out power to 6.7 million electricity customers, which the U.S. Energy Information Administration counted as 64% of every account in the state. Two out of three. A store that can’t reach its DMS can’t write a repair order or close a deal, and it can’t run payroll for the people who showed up anyway.

Atlantic hurricane season runs from June 1 through November 30, six months out of twelve, so a South Florida store spends half of every year inside it. We treat that as a date on the IT calendar. Before June, a backup gets restored for real, on a real machine, with somebody from the store watching, because the morning after landfall is a terrible time to learn that the backup was bad all along. The phone system gets a rule for where calls go when a building has no power. And each group decides, in writing, which rooftop reopens first. Two older posts of ours cover the basics, one on data backup best practices and one on what an incident response plan does for a dealership.

Helion supports dealerships in South Florida today. Three Rules a Miami Dealership Answers To

Florida did pass a privacy law. Good news, mostly. It just wasn’t written with a car dealer in mind. That leaves one federal rule carrying most of the weight, and a state breach statute with one of the shorter deadlines in the country sitting right behind it. Here are all three.

RULE	WHAT IT IS	DATE OR DEADLINE	AT THE STORE
FTC Safeguards Rule	
Federal rule for dealers that arrange financing or leasing
Applies	Amended rule in force June 9, 2023	A written security program, a qualified individual, multi-factor authentication, encryption, testing and vendor oversight. Get the checklist
Florida Digital Bill of Rights	
State privacy law aimed at very large technology companies
Narrow law	In force July 1, 2024	Reaches companies above $1 billion in global revenue that also pass a second test, such as earning half that revenue from selling ads.
Florida Information Protection Act	
Florida Statutes, Section 501.171
Applies	30 days to notify people	Tell the Department of Legal Affairs within 30 days when 500 or more Floridians are affected. Credit bureaus too, above 1,000.

Why doesn’t the privacy law reach a dealership? Look at how the statute defines a controller. A company has to bring in more than $1 billion a year in global gross revenue, and then it also has to earn at least half of that from selling ads, or run a smart speaker with a voice assistant, or operate an app store with 250,000 or more apps. A big public dealer group can clear the first bar. Not the second. That one describes a different business entirely.

The breach law is the one to respect. Under Section 501.171, any business holding Floridians’ personal information has to take reasonable measures to protect it and has 30 days to send notices after a breach, and the Safeguards Rule in 16 CFR 314.4 adds a written security program and a notice to the FTC within 30 days at 500 or more consumers. Two clocks, one incident. Ask your attorney how each one applies. Our dealership compliance team runs both out of one program. Stores in more than one state? Dealership Cybersecurity in Miami and Fort Lauderdale

June 2024 put a price on a vendor outage. AutoNation, the public dealer group headquartered in Fort Lauderdale, said in the earnings release it filed with the SEC that the CDK Global outage cut its second-quarter earnings by an estimated $1.55 per share, with $0.79 of that in one-time costs. New and used volumes took the hit. So did finance and insurance. Parts and service too. We said it then and we’d say it again. CDK wasn’t a one-time wake-up call.

No store can stop a vendor from going down. What a store can control is its own side of the wire, and that’s where our dealership cybersecurity work lives. A live, US-based security operations center watches the network day and night. Multi-factor authentication goes on email and remote access. Backups get that test restore we mentioned, because a backup nobody has ever restored is only a hope. The wider picture is in the state of dealership cybersecurity.
