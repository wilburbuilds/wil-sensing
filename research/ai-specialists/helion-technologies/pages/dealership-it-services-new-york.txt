Dealership IT Services in New York Cross Three State Lines

A dealer group in this metro can have a store on Northern Boulevard in Queens, another on Route 17 in Paramus and a third off the Post Road in Stamford, and that’s three states before lunch. Three attorneys general, three breach laws, one group. It adds up. The DMS, the phones and the logins are still supposed to work the same way at every one of them. Usually they don’t.

That gap is where we start. Most groups here grew by buying stores, and every store they bought came with its own vendor, its own firewall and its own way of doing things. Nobody planned it. It just piled up.

We like that kind of problem. Helion Technologies has supported automotive and heavy truck dealerships, and nobody else, since 1997. This page is part of our dealership IT services by location hub, and it covers what’s different about running stores around New York.

Field technicians are dispatched for a dead switch, new cabling or a store moving to a bigger lot on Long Island.
Watched around the clock
A US-based team monitors networks, servers and security alerts 24/7, including the hours your stores are closed.

Our dealership help desk is in Baltimore, same time zone as you. Whenever your New York metro stores are open, 7 days a week, the help desk is open too. The service drive in Hempstead opens at seven? We’re on. The last F&I deal in Edison runs past nine on a Saturday night? Still here. We close for Thanksgiving and Christmas and that’s it. After-hours calls, weekends and field dispatch come inside the one monthly number, no trip charges, and the bill doesn’t jump because a problem turned out bigger than it looked. Ask any provider for the help desk metrics a dealer principal should see, get them written into a dealership IT support SLA, and compare.

One Breach, Three Clocks

Picture one phished password at a group with stores on Long Island, in Bergen County and in Greenwich. The state lines decide who you call and how long you’ve got, and they don’t agree with each other at all. Here’s the short version.

RULE	WHO HEARS FIRST	DEADLINE	AT THE STORE

New York’s deadline is the one that changed most recently. Until December 2024 the state’s breach law said only “the most expedient time possible,” and now it says 30 days, with no extra time to work out the scope. Thirty days goes quickly when nobody kept the logs. Ask anyone who’s tried. The SHIELD Act’s safeguards rule sits alongside it, and a dealership that’s governed by and complies with the Gramm-Leach-Bliley rules is treated as meeting that part. Connecticut’s Attorney General takes breach reports online and has sent warning letters over slow notices.

Privacy laws are a separate question. New Jersey’s exempts a dealership as a whole, and Connecticut’s, since July 1, 2026, exempts only the data the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act covers, which our Philadelphia and Boston pages lay out. Your attorney decides how each rule applies to you, and our dealership compliance team keeps one written program that answers to all of them. Read what dealerships need to know about the SHIELD Act, start with a Safeguards risk assessment template, or compare states in dealership privacy laws by state.

Dealership Cybersecurity in New York

The attack we worry about most here doesn’t start with code. It starts with a phone call to a busy store. Someone says they’re from IT, knows the GM’s name and the DMS you run, and needs the service manager to read back the code that just arrived on their phone. It sounds routine. It isn’t. On a Saturday with a full showroom, it often works, and that one code can open email, remote access and the customer files behind them. One code. That is all it takes.

CDK Global’s 2024 survey of dealers found that 35% had a cyberattack or incident that year, and 92% of them said it did real financial or operational damage. Our dealership cybersecurity work covers the store’s side. A live, US-based security operations center watches the network through managed detection and response, advanced endpoint detection and response guards the computers, and Microsoft 365 security closes the inbox where most attacks start. Security awareness training teaches staff that real IT never asks for a code, and penetration testing shows where an attacker would get in.

Our older piece on vishing covers the phone side, and AI email assistants covers the newest way inboxes leak. Want to know where your logins, backups and phone procedures stand? Start with a complimentary IT and cybersecurity assessment. We’ll write the gaps down.
