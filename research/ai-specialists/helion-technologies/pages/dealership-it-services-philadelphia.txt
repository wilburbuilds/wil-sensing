One Metro, Three State Privacy Rulebooks

The FTC Safeguards Rule covers every dealer that arranges financing or leasing, on either bank of the Delaware. That part never moves. What changes at the state line is the privacy law stacked on top of it, and one of the three rows below is about to change again.

JURISDICTION	COMPREHENSIVE PRIVACY LAW	IN FORCE	AT THE STORE
Pennsylvania	
No comprehensive consumer privacy law. House Bill 78 is pending in the Senate
No state law	None yet	The FTC Safeguards Rule and the state breach notification law are the standards to meet.
New Jersey	
New Jersey Data Privacy Act (NJDPA)
Dealership exempt	January 15, 2025	Financial institutions under the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act are exempt as whole entities.
Delaware	
Delaware Personal Data Privacy Act (DPDPA)
Changes in 2027	January 1, 2025	The whole-entity exemption narrows to banks, credit unions and insurers on January 1, 2027.

Delaware is the row to watch. When the law took effect, any business covered by the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act was exempt as a whole, and a dealership that arranges financing counted. House Bill 380 rewrites that. Starting January 1, 2027, the whole-entity pass goes only to named types of financial institutions, and the law firm Morgan Lewis, in its October 2026 summary of the amendment, tells every other business covered by that federal law to check whether it’s still exempt. The same bill drops the coverage threshold from 35,000 Delaware consumers to 10,000. Data already regulated under the federal law stays out. Ask your attorney how your Delaware stores land. Maryland went through its own version of this in 2025, and we covered it in why Maryland’s data privacy law matters for dealers everywhere.

Pennsylvania is quieter, for now. House Bill 78 passed the state House 127 to 76 in October 2025 and reached second consideration in the Senate in June 2026, and it hadn’t become law when this page was last updated. What a Pennsylvania store does answer to is the state’s Breach of Personal Information Notification Act. Since September 26, 2024, a breach touching more than 500 Pennsylvania residents means notifying the attorney general at the same time as the customers, and 12 months of credit monitoring when Social Security, bank account or driver’s license numbers were exposed, according to BakerHostetler’s 2024 review of the amendment.

None of this shrinks the federal job. The Safeguards Rule still expects a written security program, plus a notice to the FTC within 30 days when a breach reaches 500 or more consumers. Our FTC Safeguards Rule checklist walks through it line by line. The rule itself, 16 CFR 314.4, is a short read. Our dealership compliance team keeps one program that holds up in all three states.

Dealership Cybersecurity in Philadelphia, PA

This one is close to home. Two dealerships in the Philadelphia suburbs have filed ransomware notices with state attorneys general since 2025. A store in lower Bucks County found an intruder on its network in late February 2025, and by the time the review of the stolen files wrapped up in June 2026 it had 5,450 people to notify. A second store, in Chester County, spotted unusual activity that November and sent letters to 1,909 people the following May. Neither is a big operation. Both made the list. After the 2024 outage at CDK Global we wrote that CDK wasn’t a one-time wake-up call. It wasn’t.

Nobody can promise a store won’t be next. What a store can control is how early an intruder gets caught and how fast the systems come back, and that’s where our dealership cybersecurity work lives. A live, US-based security operations center watches the network day and night. Multi-factor authentication goes on email and remote access. Backups get a test restore, because a backup nobody has ever restored is only a hope. It’s the same reason dealerships are rethinking in-house cybersecurity.

Not sure where your stores stand? A complimentary IT and cybersecurity assessment puts the answer in writing. Your manufacturer has a say as well, so check the OEM cybersecurity requirements for your brands.
