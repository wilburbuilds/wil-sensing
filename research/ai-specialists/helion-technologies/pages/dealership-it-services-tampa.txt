Helene and Milton Hit Tampa Bay 13 Days Apart

Helene never made landfall here. It came ashore in the Big Bend on the night of September 26, 2024, and it still pushed the water 6.31 feet above the normal high-tide line at the St. Petersburg tide gauge, a station that has kept records since 1947 and had never seen more than the 3.97 feet of Hurricane Elena in 1985, according to the National Hurricane Center’s report on the storm. Then came Milton. It made landfall near Siesta Key on October 9 as a Category 3 storm, and by the next afternoon the U.S. Department of Energy counted a peak of 3.4 million Florida power customers in the dark.

Wind gets the headlines. Here, water does the damage. A switch sitting on the floor of a ground-level closet in Pinellas doesn’t need a roof to come off. Six inches of seawater will do it. So the IT plan for a store near the bay starts with height and distance, which means the network and phone gear go up off the floor and the backups live somewhere that can’t flood along with the building. Height first. Before June 1, one of those backups gets restored for real, start to finish. Need a starting point? If the DMS is what goes dark, our DMS outage plan covers the first hours.

Florida Rules a Tampa Bay Dealership Answers To

Three rules matter here. One is federal and does most of the work. One is a state breach law with a 30-day clock. The third gets the most press and almost never reaches a car store. Groups with rooftops on both coasts will find the same three on our Miami page, because the state line doesn’t move.

RULE	WHAT IT IS	DATE OR DEADLINE	AT THE STORE
FTC Safeguards Rule	
Federal rule for dealers that arrange financing or leasing
Applies	Amended rule in force June 9, 2023	A written security program, a qualified individual, multi-factor authentication, encryption, testing and vendor oversight. Get the checklist
Florida Information Protection Act	
Florida Statutes, Section 501.171
Applies	30 days to notify people	Notify the Department of Legal Affairs within 30 days when 500 or more Floridians are affected, and the credit bureaus above 1,000.
Florida Digital Bill of Rights	
State privacy law written for very large technology companies
Narrow law	In force July 1, 2024	Covers companies above $1 billion in global revenue that also pass a second test, such as earning half that revenue from ads.

Start with the one that applies. Section 501.171 covers any business holding Floridians’ personal information, so it covers every dealership in Hillsborough and Pinellas, and it asks for reasonable measures to protect that data and notices within 30 days of a breach. The federal Safeguards Rule in 16 CFR 314.4 runs right alongside it, with a written security program and its own 30-day notice to the FTC once 500 or more consumers are involved. One breach can start both clocks on the same morning. Plan for both.

And the privacy law? Read how the statute defines a controller. A company needs more than $1 billion in yearly global revenue and then a second trait, like half its revenue from ads, a smart speaker with a voice assistant or an app store with 250,000 or more apps. That’s not a dealer group. Your attorney decides how each rule applies to you, and our dealership compliance team keeps the program that answers both. Stores in other states as well? Dealership Cybersecurity in Tampa Bay

The phishing shows up with the cleanup crews. On September 25, 2024, the day before Helene reached Florida, the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency warned that emails and social posts tied to a hurricane often carry malicious links or attachments. After a storm, inboxes fill with relief money, insurance forms and FEMA paperwork. A tired office manager is exactly who those messages are written for. One click is enough.

Storms only add to it. CDK Global’s 2024 survey of dealers found that 35% had a cyberattack or incident that year, and 92% of them said it did real financial or operational damage. Our dealership cybersecurity work covers the store’s side of that. A live, US-based security operations center watches the network day and night through managed detection and response, advanced endpoint detection and response guards the computers, and multi-factor authentication goes on email and remote access for every person at the store who uses them. The basics on preventing phishing at a dealership still hold up, Microsoft 365 security closes the inbox most of those emails land in, and the state of dealership cybersecurity shows the bigger picture.
