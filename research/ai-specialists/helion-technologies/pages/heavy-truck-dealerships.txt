That's a real gap, not a minor one.

Search for a managed IT provider that specifically serves heavy truck dealerships, and the results thin out fast. Most providers in this space market to automotive dealerships and treat heavy truck operations, if they mention them at all, as an afterthought bolted onto the same generic pitch.

A heavy truck dealership runs on the same kind of DMS-dependent, compliance-heavy operation as an automotive dealership, often with:

 Rural locations spread further apart
 A service department carrying more weight relative to sales than a typical car dealership

Network outages hit differently when the nearest backup technician is hours away, not a short drive across town.

Helion's Managed IT model wasn't built as an automotive product with heavy truck bolted on afterward. It's the same standard, applied to both. Choosing a new DMS? A Different Kind of Buyer, Not Just a Different Location

Business to business

Heavy truck dealerships aren't consumer-facing the way automotive dealerships are, and that changes more than the sales floor. Heavy truck dealers are business people selling to other businesses:

Trucks Fleet maintenance contracts Ongoing service relationships

Because that's how they sell, it's also how they evaluate who they work with. A vendor pitch built for a consumer-facing dealership doesn't land the same way with an audience that thinks in terms of partnership first.

A consolidated vertical

The vertical has also consolidated significantly. There are still independent one, two, and three-store operations, but a large share of heavy truck dealerships now operate as groups of 10, 15, 20, or more locations.

1–3 stores
10–20+ stores

Run with the same operational sophistication as a multi-rooftop automotive group. Helion's model was built to fit that scale.
