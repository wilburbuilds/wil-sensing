IT STAFFING COMPARISON

In-House vs Managed vs Co-Managed IT for Dealerships

What each model covers, what it really costs and what happens the day someone leaves, whether you hire IT staff, hand it to a partner or split the work.

Get Your Complimentary IT & Cybersecurity Assessment →

Dealerships weighing in-house vs outsourced IT really have three models to choose from. Hire your own IT staff, hand everything to a managed partner, or go co-managed, where your IT person stays in charge and a partner team covers the specialties one hire can’t.

Last updated: October 15, 2026

IT has split into specialties. One hire can’t be the whole bench.

Erik Nachbahr founded Helion Technologies in 1997, and he’s sat across the desk from a lot of dealer principals weighing this exact call. His test for a store that runs on one IT person is blunt: “Is he gonna walk out the door, and then where does that leave you?” That isn’t a knock on the person. It’s math.

Search for in-house vs managed IT and you’ll mostly find managed service providers arguing for themselves. We’re one of them, so this page compares all three models on the same questions, including the one where your IT manager stays on payroll and keeps running things. We’ll be straight about it. For a lot of stores that’s the right answer, and our co-managed IT page covers how it works day to day. If you’d rather hand over all of it, start with managed IT support.

Full disclosure. Helion sells fully managed and co-managed IT, and some of our longest relationships run through an internal IT director. We’re not here to talk anyone out of their IT person.

Why One IT Hire Costs More Than the Salary Line

Start with what the market pays. A network administrator and a security analyst are two different jobs now, with two different salaries, and a store that wants both skills on payroll is buying both before it pays for benefits, training, tools or a single certification renewal. It adds up fast.

Security is where it gets tight. The Bureau of Labor Statistics projects information security analyst jobs to grow 21% from 2025 to 2035, so the person who can actually read an alert at 2 a.m. is the person every bank and hospital in your town is trying to hire too. That’s a hard hire.

Big groups feel it as well. Even a dealer group with a chief information officer buys outside depth, which tells you this isn’t a small-store problem. That’s dealership IT now.

Good IT people are worth it. They’re also scarce.

May 2025

The Bureau of Labor Statistics put median pay for network and computer systems administrators at $99,130, and for information security analysts at $129,180.

Two specialties. Two salaries.

February 2026

One of the largest heavy truck dealer groups in the country, with 126 franchised truck centers in 23 states and a CIO leading its security program, says in its 2025 annual report to the SEC that it uses “a combination of internal resources and external managed security service providers.”

Even the biggest groups split the work.

BEFORE YOU DECIDE

Six Questions to Answer Before You Hire, Outsource or Split It

The salary is the easy number. These six decide whether a model holds up on a bad Saturday, so answer them in writing, with your controller in the room.

Dealership IT staffing review
Same six questions, any model

1 What happens the day your IT person leaves?

It happens. People take the job down the street, burn out or retire, and every so often one really does win the lottery, which is the point of our older post on what happens when your IT guy wins the lottery. With a team behind the store, as Erik puts it, “you’re not one heartbeat from the ground.”

List what only one person knows today.

2 Who’s watching at 2 a.m. on a Saturday?

Attackers don’t keep store hours. One person can’t watch a network around the clock and still sleep, so ask every model you’re weighing who sees the alert, who’s allowed to act on it and how fast they do. If the honest answer is that it emails somebody, that’s your answer.

Get the after-hours escalation path in writing.

3 Who owns the Safeguards file?

A store that arranges financing needs a Qualified Individual, a written risk assessment, a penetration test every year, vulnerability scans every six months and a yearly report to the board under the FTC Safeguards Rule. It’s its own specialty. An in-house manager can own it, if somebody gives them the hours.

Find the date of your last penetration test.

4 Who does the strategy, and who does the daily work?

They’re two different jobs that usually land on one desk. Erik describes the hybrid as “somebody working on your strategy … and the day to day blocking and tackling is handled by somebody else.” Both halves matter. Either side can take either half, including projects like a DMS transition.

Ask your IT person which half they want.

5 What does it really cost, all in?

Add the salary, benefits, training, certification renewals and the cost of covering a two-week vacation, then put every IT, phone and security invoice you pay now next to it. Be honest about it. One dealer group that shrank from eight stores to three lost its whole internal IT team overnight, CIO included. Its cost comparison showed outsourcing was more cost-competitive than hiring two full-time replacements. More stories like it are in our case studies.

Put every invoice on one table.

6 Does your IT person want help?

Co-managed only works when the internal person wants it. Not every IT manager does. Two of our longest-running co-managed relationships, groups of more than 1,000 seats each, were built that way from the start. Their IT director runs the relationship, a smaller internal crew handles the field work and Helion carries accountability and risk behind them.

Ask them first. Before anyone else.

How Much IT Work Stays on Your Payroll in Each Model

The dots show how much of the IT work your own staff carry.

1
Go fully managed

One team owns all of it, with one contact for leadership and no IT hire to replace.

2
Go co-managed

Your IT person stays in charge, and a partner team covers security, compliance and the depth behind them.

3
Keep it all in-house

Full control and people who know every rooftop. It holds up best as a department, not one person.

None of them is wrong. A single-point store where the controller doubles as the IT person weighs this very differently than a 20-rooftop group with an IT director and a help desk of its own, which is why our IT services for dealer groups start from the team you already have. For leadership, Erik frames the first conversation as a second opinion, not a pitch: “Do you want a second look at how your money’s getting spent?”
