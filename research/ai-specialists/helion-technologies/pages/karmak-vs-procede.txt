Karmak vs Procede: A Heavy Truck DMS Comparison From the IT Side

Fusion, Blaze and Excede compared on where they run, which truck brands they connect to, and what each one asks of your network.

Karmak and Procede both build dealer management systems for heavy truck dealers. Karmak sells Fusion and, since buying DSI, Blaze. Procede sells one platform, Excede. The biggest IT difference is where each one runs and who keeps it running.

 One DMS under all of it
Heavy truck has three big DMS names. This page covers two of them.

Erik Nachbahr, who founded Helion Technologies in 1997, sums up the market in one line. “Heavy truck is CDK, Karmak, and Procede Software. Those are the big ones there.” He’s right. CDK sells to car and truck stores alike, while Karmak and Procede were built for commercial vehicles from the start. Two specialists. One generalist.

Search for a straight Karmak vs Procede comparison and you mostly get software directories. Nobody covers the IT side. So this page does, for the stores Helion supports through its heavy truck dealership IT program. A demo shows you what a repair order looks like on screen. It won’t show you what happens to that repair order when the circuit at a store two hours from the nearest tech goes dark on a Thursday afternoon with three trucks waiting on parts. That part’s on us.

Full disclosure. Karmak signed a reseller agreement with Helion back in 2018, so we know Karmak well. That’s why the comparison below sticks to what each vendor publishes.

Why Karmak vs Procede Is a Live Question in 2026

Both vendors changed shape in the past year. Neither change forces a dealer to do anything. Nothing’s on fire. Both are still good reasons to reread your contract before the next renewal lands on your desk.

Karmak’s deal was the bigger market move, since it now sells two DMS products into the same PACCAR stores and says both will keep being sold and supported for the customers already running them.

Procede took outside money to build faster, with AI reporting it calls Procede Intelligence and more integrations across the commercial vehicle world on the way, according to its January announcement. On Blaze and thinking about Fusion? Our IT checklist for the Karmak DSI deal covers that one path in detail.

New owners. Same login screen.

Karmak acquired DSI Solutions, maker of the Blaze DMS, adding more than 700 account locations. Karmak says the deal gives it over 50% of the DMS market among PACCAR dealers.

One vendor, two products, half the PACCAR stores.

Same Excede. New money behind the roadmap.

Before the demo

Six IT Questions to Settle Before You Pick Karmak or Procede

The demo covers screens and workflows. These are the questions it skips. Ask both vendors the same six, in writing. Then lay the answers side by side.

Want the IT side of either move priced out for your stores? Request a price quote.

Where Helion fits

The network, the devices, the security and the Safeguards file under the DMS. That work doesn’t change much with the logo on the login screen. Where the risk sits does.

Heavy truck DMS evaluation Same six questions for both vendors

1 Who runs the server?

Fusion can sit on a server in your building, and then your team patches it, backs it up and keeps it powered. Somebody has to. Hosted Fusion and Excede on Azure hand that job to the vendor.

Ask for the backup schedule and the date of the last restore test.

2 What happens when the internet drops?

A hosted or browser-based DMS stops at any rooftop that loses its circuit. A server in one store keeps that store running and can cut off every other one. Both happen.

Price cellular failover into the move, store by store.

3 Which OEM links do you really use?

Both vendors publish long OEM lists. What counts is the handful your people touch every day for parts ordering, warranty claims and labor reporting.

Bring your make list. A Freightliner and Isuzu store needs different things than a Kenworth-only one.

4 Which devices have to change?

Label printers, signature pads, scanners and shop tablets all talk to the DMS. A Windows client and a web browser make different demands on every one of them.

Count them. Every rooftop, before anybody sets a go-live date.

5 What does the vendor owe you on security?

A truck dealer that finances or leases falls under the FTC Safeguards Rule, which says to pick service providers that can protect customer data and check on them over time. Both vendors changed ownership structure in the last year.

Get current security documentation from whichever one you choose.

6 How does your data come back out?

Contracts end. Groups sell stores. Ask what format your history leaves in, how many days you get to pull it, and who at the vendor signs for that answer.

Get it on paper before the demo ends.

Three Ways a Heavy Truck DMS Can Run

The dots show how much of the IT work stays at your store.

1
In the browser

Blaze works this way. The lightest footprint at the store, and the most dependent on a steady connection.

2
Vendor-hosted

Fusion on Xcelerate, or Excede on Azure. The vendor runs the server while your circuits carry every transaction.

3
On your own server

Fusion’s on-premise option. The most control, and the patching, backups and power are yours to own.

None is wrong. A 12-store group with stores hours apart weighs them differently than a single rooftop off the interstate with one IT person, one internet circuit and a server closet that doubles as the copier room. Our DMS transition support plans the move for whichever model you land on.
