Microsoft Runs the Cloud. Your Tenant Is Still Yours.

Microsoft’s own shared responsibility model is blunt about it. For software like Microsoft 365, Microsoft looks after the datacenters, the servers and the network. The customer always keeps four jobs, the data, the devices, the accounts and the access controls, multi-factor authentication included. That customer is the dealership. Plenty of stores bought the licenses and assumed the rest came in the box.

Managed Microsoft 365 is how Helion Technologies picks up those four jobs as part of managed IT for dealerships, run by the same team that handles your cybersecurity and FTC Safeguards compliance. It’s ongoing work. If you think someone is already inside your mailboxes, start with the Microsoft 365 breach assessment, which is a one-time hunt for signs of an intruder. This page is about who runs the tenant every day after that.

Buying Microsoft 365 isn’t the same as running it. We run it, for every store, every day.

SHARED RESPONSIBILITY

FROM THE FIELD

What Erik Tells Dealers About Microsoft 365

We asked Erik Nachbahr, Helion’s founder and a CISSP, what dealers get wrong about Microsoft 365. His four answers follow, in his own words. Not one of them is about a missing feature. Every one of them is about who’s actually running the thing, and whether anyone checked what it can reach.

WORRIED SOMEONE’S ALREADY IN?

Then start with the one-time Microsoft 365 breach assessment. It looks for signs of an intruder before anyone talks about running the tenant.

Four things Erik tells dealers
Erik Nachbahr, Helion founder

Backup Retained isn’t backed up

“Microsoft doesn’t guarantee you backups of all of your data. It retains it for a bit, but that’s not a backup and restore, like what you typically expect.” A deleted email waits 14 days by default. A deleted file waits 93. After that, it’s gone.

Retention isn’t a restore.

Rights Permissions that were already too wide

“In dealerships where their networks are less secure, and they tend to have issues with people having permissions to things they shouldn’t already, when you add AI, now you’re spreading that even further.” A new hire’s account gets copied from whoever had the job before, the old access rides along with it, and three job changes later a salesperson can open the accounting share and the F&I manager’s mailbox without anyone deciding they should. Nobody notices, because nobody looks.

Messy rights travel.

Copilot Available isn’t the same as recommended

“Should they even be using Microsoft Copilot? No. Can you use it? Yes.” Helion gets dealers Copilot licenses when they ask. The order matters, though. Microsoft’s own Copilot privacy page says it surfaces whatever data each person already has permission to view, so a messy tenant gives you a very well-informed Copilot. Our post on what to lock down before you turn Copilot on walks through it.

Licenses on request. Cleanup first.

Access AI plugged in with admin rights

“Oftentimes AI is connected in with some high level administrative access. So all of that role-based security you have in place to control what types of things people are able to access, now they can access everything.” It usually takes one click. An app or an AI tool asks for permission to read every mailbox and every file in the tenant, someone who happens to have admin rights approves it in between two other jobs, and that’s that. The app is now one more identity in your tenant, which is why AI and machine identities need an owner too.

Microsoft 365 Backup Is Still Your Job

Moving to the cloud made backups feel old-fashioned. “Most dealers’ technology is cloud-based now,” Erik says, “so they just don’t think about backups at all.” Email moved to Exchange Online. The shared drive turned into SharePoint and OneDrive. Nobody set up the copy.

Microsoft’s safety nets are real, and they’re short. They catch a file someone deleted last week, which is handy, but they’re a thin net when ransomware scrambles a synced folder, when a departing employee empties a mailbox and nobody notices for a month, or when the controller needs last quarter’s version of a spreadsheet that’s been saved over 40 times since. We go deeper on it in whether you’re properly backing up Microsoft 365. For servers in Azure and everything else the store runs, see backup and disaster recovery for dealerships.

Retention isn’t a restore.

What Microsoft keeps

Deleted email for 14 days by default, 30 at most. Deleted OneDrive and SharePoint files for 93 days, counted across both recycle bins. Then Microsoft purges them for good.

A short safety net.

What a managed backup adds

A separate copy of your mailboxes, OneDrive and SharePoint, kept outside the tenant, so a mailbox or a folder can be rolled back to a point before the damage happened.

Your copy, on your terms.
