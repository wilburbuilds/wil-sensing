The AI Is Already in Your Store. The Chat History Isn’t Yours.

Walk the service drive or the BDC on a weekday and you’ll find somebody asking an AI tool to clean up a customer email or boil down a long note. Most of it happens on personal accounts. Nobody asked IT. Cox Automotive’s 2026 AI tracker found that 82% of dealers already use AI, and that only counts the use a dealer knows about. The rest lives in browser tabs the store doesn’t control, holding customer names, payoff amounts and whatever else got pasted in that day.

Secure AI Chat is the chat workspace inside Helion’s Managed AI program, the fifth pillar next to managed IT, cybersecurity, compliance and Tekion enablement. That page covers the whole program, from the governance it feeds to the service tiers. This one is about the chat itself: what your staff get when they open it, and what your managers get to see.

Staff will use AI either way. What matters is whose account it runs on.

Public AI Tools, a Block, or a Workspace You Govern

Once leadership notices how much AI the store is using, there are usually two reactions. One is to let it ride, since people really are getting work done faster. The other is to block the chatbot sites at the firewall and call it handled. We’ve watched both play out, and neither one makes the data question go away.

Blocking is the one that surprises people. The firewall shuts the sites off on store PCs, and then the same advisor pulls out a personal phone in the service drive and carries on, except now there’s no record of anything at all. The third option is the one we run: give staff a better tool than the free one, on an account the dealership owns, and the reason to work around IT goes away on its own.

The FTC has warned AI companies that customers may reveal sensitive or confidential information when they use AI models. At a dealership that isn’t abstract. It’s a credit application, a trade payoff, a customer’s cell number. Our guide to public AI and customer PII walks through what’s actually at stake when that data lands in a public tool.

A block hides shadow AI. It doesn’t end it.

Public AI tools

Staff use them on their own accounts. They’re fast, they’re free, and the dealership has no record of what went in.

You find out last.

Blocking AI

We’ve seen plenty of stores try it. The sites stop loading on store PCs, and people switch to their phones.

Then you know even less.

A governed workspace

The models your people already like, on an account you own. Access goes by role, and every prompt gets logged.

That’s the one we run.

INSIDE THE WORKSPACE

What Your Staff Get When They Open Secure AI Chat

Your people won’t need a class for this. If they’ve typed a question into ChatGPT on their phone, they already know how it works. What’s different is who owns it. The account belongs to the dealership, we set the rules with you, and the models are picked for the work instead of whatever free site somebody bookmarked.

WRITE THE RULES FIRST

Decide what staff can and can’t paste into AI before the first person signs in. Our dealership AI use policy template is a free place to start, and the access settings in the workspace follow what it says.

Secure AI Chat, piece by piece
What staff use and what managers see

Models GPT, Claude, Gemini and others in one window

Some models are better at a follow-up email to a customer. Others do better with a 40-page PDF of warranty terms. Staff choose from GPT, Claude, Gemini and others in one private window, and when a better model comes along we add it there, so nobody retrains and you aren’t stuck with one AI vendor. That matters.

Switch models, not habits.

Phones The same workspace on iOS and Android

Salespeople are out on the lot, advisors are on the drive, and managers spend half the week between stores. The mobile app carries the same sign-in, the same controls and the same history, so a question asked from the parking lot gets the same protection as one typed at a desk. It’s also the real answer to the personal phone problem that blocking creates.

Same rules in the pocket.

Access Role-based access that Helion manages

Who can use the workspace, and how AI behaves for each group, is set by role and managed by Helion along with the rest of your accounts. One list, one owner. When someone leaves, their AI access goes out the door with the rest of their offboarding instead of living on in a personal login. Rex Marchant’s guide to least-privilege access for AI tools explains why each role should reach only what it needs.

Gone with the badge.

Oversight Adoption reporting and an audit trail

Leadership sees who is using AI, how often and for what, which beats walking the floor and guessing whether the rollout took or whether everyone quietly went back to the free app on their phone. Every prompt and response goes into an audit trail, so when somebody asks what was shared and by whom, there’s an answer on file. That’s the point. Cox found that about 1 in 3 dealers either aren’t measuring AI’s impact or have no clarity on how they measure it.

A record, not a rumor.

Your name Your dealership’s name and logo on it

Staff sign in to something that looks like a store tool, because it is one. It’s yours. Every model call runs with zero data retention, so the AI providers don’t keep the conversation or train public models on it, and client records and financials stay in your environment. The Managed AI page covers the isolation and retention terms in full.

A company tool, not a third-party app.

A chat window on its own won’t do that. Setup matters. Somebody still has to set it up for each department, watch how it’s used and adjust, and that’s the part we take on.
