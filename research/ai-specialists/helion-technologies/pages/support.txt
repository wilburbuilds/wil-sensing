Managed IT for dealerships means outsourcing network monitoring, helpdesk support, and DMS uptime to a provider that understands dealership-specific systems. Helion Technologies delivers this exclusively for automotive and heavy truck dealerships, with an 82-second average response time and 98% client retention.

A DMS outage doesn't just slow things down. It stops sales from closing and service advisors from checking cars in.

Every minute a dealership's systems are down is a minute the sales floor and service lane aren't making money, and a tested backup and disaster recovery plan decides how many of those minutes there are.

There are usually two ways dealerships handle this. One internal hire tries to cover network issues, vendor calls, and security all at once. Or a generalist MSP treats the dealership like any other small business. Neither approach accounts for what actually runs a dealership: a DMS platform, OEM-specific network requirements, and compliance rules that don't apply to a law firm or a dentist's office down the street. Our comparison of in-house, managed and co-managed IT weighs the options line by line.

Helion built its entire model around dealership IT specifically, not IT in general.

"Why Not Just Hire Someone In-House?"

Every dealership asks this before calling a managed IT provider.

IT has split into specialties. Network administration, cybersecurity, compliance, project management, and DMS management used to be one job. Now they're five. One internal hire can be good at two or three of those. Covering all five, all the time, without ever taking a vacation or calling in sick, isn't realistic for one person.

That's not a knock on internal IT staff. It's math. A team behind one standard covers more ground than any single hire can, at a comparable cost to what many dealerships are already paying for partial coverage.
