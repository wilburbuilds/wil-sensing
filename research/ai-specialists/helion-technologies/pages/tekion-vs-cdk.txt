Tekion vs CDK: A DMS Comparison From the IT Side

What changes for your network, devices, phones, integrations and security when a store moves between the two, or decides to stay put.

Tekion and CDK both sell dealer management systems to franchised dealerships, and both run them as hosted platforms. For IT, the real difference shows up in a move. Phones, network services, integrations and user access all change hands.

Most of a DMS move happens below the login screen.

Erik Nachbahr, who founded Helion Technologies in 1997, has walked a lot of stores through this exact switch. “It’s such a titanic shift from where you were on CDK or Reynolds and Reynolds that nobody absorbs all of it on the first pass,” he wrote in his post on life after Tekion go-live. He meant the people. The network’s the same way.

Search for Tekion vs CDK and you mostly get feature grids and software reviews. Almost nobody covers the IT side, so this page does, for dealers looking at our Tekion enablement services or our DMS transition support. A demo shows you how a deal gets desked. It won’t show you what happens to that deal when the F&I printer can’t find the new system on the last Saturday of the month, with two customers waiting and the sales manager standing in the doorway. That part’s on us.

Full disclosure. Helion is a Tekion Certified Partner, and we support stores running CDK every day. That’s why the comparison below sticks to what each company publishes.

Why Tekion vs CDK Is a Live Question in 2026

Both companies had a big year. Neither one forces a dealer to do anything, and nothing here is a reason to panic. It’s a reason to know your renewal date. Write it down.

Tekion’s growth is the market story, since more franchised stores are now running its Automotive Retail Cloud and more dealer principals are being asked by their own general managers whether they’ll be next.

CDK changed leadership this month and has been rolling its products into one platform it calls the Dealership Xperience Platform. One of the largest public dealer groups in the country had moved 70% of its stores to Tekion by late July 2026. Our DMS conversion checklist covers what a move like that asks of IT.

Big news on both sides. Your contract matters more.

WHERE HELION FITS

The network, the devices, the security and the Safeguards file under the DMS. We do that work for Tekion stores and CDK stores alike.

Dealership DMS evaluation
Same six questions, either path

1 Who owns the phones?

If your phones came from CDK, changing the DMS doesn’t end that phone contract by itself. Check the term, who holds the numbers, and how calls and caller details will reach the new system, because a service advisor who can’t see who’s calling loses the thread with the customer fast. Tekion connects calling and texting through partners, so that’s a choice you get to make. Our guide to re-bidding phones and network in a CDK to Tekion move walks through it.

Pull the phone contract before you sign the DMS one.

2 Who provides the network?

Some stores buy circuits, firewalls, Wi-Fi or managed network services from the same company that sells them the DMS, and those line items tend to blur together on one invoice over the years. A move turns each of those into its own decision. New circuits carry lead times. Order early.

List every network line item on the current invoice.

3 Is the internet ready to be the DMS?

Both platforms are hosted, so a rooftop that loses its circuit loses its DMS. Tekion’s Implementation Terms also put electrical work, data cabling and the readiness sign-off on the dealer. Our Tekion network requirements guide walks through it.

Pull the primary circuit and watch the store keep working.

4 Which devices have to change?

Printers, signature pads, check scanners and the PC on every desk in the building meet the new system on day one, including the impact printer in accounting that nobody has touched since the last conversion. A browser-based DMS on an out-of-support PC is still slow.

Print a real deal jacket and a real RO from every printer. Every one.

5 Which integrations come along?

F&I menus, credit bureaus, OEM parts ordering, payroll and the CRM all connect to the DMS today, and every one of them has its own vendor, its own contract and its own idea of how long a new connection should take. Each one gets rebuilt for the new platform or confirmed for the renewal. Ask every vendor whether it’s on Tekion’s Automotive Partner Cloud.

Ask who pays for each new connection.

6 Who gets access, and how does the data come out?

Build user roles from today’s org chart instead of copying old accounts, and turn on multi-factor authentication from day one. A store that arranges financing falls under the FTC Safeguards Rule, which requires MFA and oversight of service providers.

Get the export format and dates in writing.

3
Convert a group in waves

The same work at every rooftop, sequenced store by store, with each wave learning from the last one.

None of them is wrong. A single-point store with one controller and a server closet that doubles as the copier room weighs these very differently than a 20-rooftop group with an IT director and three states of circuits to replace.
