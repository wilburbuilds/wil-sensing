This week: the world-record pumpkin that held the title for 7 days, men jousting on rental e-bikes, and in the Hall of Fame, the Frenchman who ate an entire Cessna 150 (it's a freaking PLANE). Each newsletter has its own design system: palette, typography, masthead, callout styles, even its own rating widget (the WTF-O-METER™). The footer of each one cross-promotes the other two. **How it works (one prompt in Claude Code: "send this week's WTF")** • Research: Claude Code's built-in web search plus a page fetcher. Every candidate story goes into a research.json audit trail with its source URL, so I can always trace where a number came from. • Writing: issue.json is the single source of truth. I learned that AI-drawn bar heights are only roughly proportional, and for a data-heavy newsletter that isn't good enough. • Email: Jinja templates produce bulletproof table-based HTML. It goes out from my personal Gmail over SMTP with an App Password, images inline as CID attachments, under Gmail's 102 KB clipping limit. **Lessons** 1. Separate the AI from the arithmetic. Let Nano Banana make it beautiful; let code make it correct. 2. The Gmail API strips images from drafts. Real SMTP with inline attachments doesn't. 3. Day 6 - Scheduled automation
Note: I can’t share any real data from this build. Everything below describes how it works. I built a weekly task that gives me two things in one go: a performance report and a hygiene check on how the team keeps its records. It runs on its own, once a week. Why I built it: I oversee four teams, each with a manager and reps. Our process is very manual. Reps log their own meetings in trackers and lead sheets and nothing checks whether they’re being kept up. Checking meant opening a lot of files by hand, which took a long time so it kept slipping. The performance report: - The month’s numbers for every rep, every team and the whole region: meetings booked, meetings sat, show rate and opportunities. - A check on whether the account executives are getting a fair share of the meetings, with gaps flagged. The hygiene check - for every rep, it checks that: - Their trackers are up to date in full, with no missing fields and no impossible dates. - Meetings in the past have an outcome recorded - Every meeting has a lead sheet I get a draft per team, ready to forward to each manager, plus a summary for me. Nothing is sent without me. What took the time: It was writing the instructions and pointing Claude to exactly where to go: which folders, which tabs, which files to read and which to ignore, who is on each team and what counts as a problem. The self-improving part: Mine reads a short status file (memory file) at the start and proposes an updated version at the end. It can’t write the file itself and I approve every update. That way it gets better each week without drifting on its own. Model used: Sonnet 5.5 - ran perfectly first time!

Lead Qualification & Appointment Booking System for a Plumbing Business
I’ve been building an end-to-end automation system for a plumbing business using n8n, Google Gemini, Google Sheets, Google Calendar, and Gmail. The goal was to build a system that can collect incoming leads, understand their plumbing issues, identify urgent cases, check appointment availability, book appointments, and follow up with leads who haven’t booked yet. This is a working demo project that I built and tested to understand how these tools work together in a real business workflow. # What the system does - Lead collection: Captures customer details through a webhook form. - AI lead qualification: Uses Google Gemini to analyze the customer's plumbing issue and classify its urgency. - Lead management: Saves customer information, issue details, urgency, and lead status in Google Sheets. - Appointment booking: Checks availability in Google Calendar and creates an event when the requested slot is available. - Booking confirmation: Sends a confirmation email to the customer through Gmail. - Urgent lead alerts: Identifies urgent plumbing issues and routes them through a separate notification branch. - Automated follow-up: Checks eligible leads and sends follow-up emails to leads who haven't booked yet, while recording the follow-up timestamp to prevent repeated follow-ups. # What I tested - Customer details were saved in Google Sheets. - Gemini generated the lead qualification output. - Available appointment slots created calendar events. - Unavailable slots followed the alternative branch without creating an event. - Successful bookings updated the lead record and sent a confirmation email. - Urgent leads followed the urgent notification branch. - The follow-up workflow checked eligibility and dates, sent follow-up emails, and recorded the follow-up timestamp. 🚀 What I want to improve next(In Version 2): 1. SMS notifications — top priority I want to add SMS notifications so the plumbing business owner can receive urgent lead alerts directly on their phone, instead of relying only on email. This would make urgent requests easier to notice and respond to quickly.
