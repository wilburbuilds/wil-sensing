## What Wil should steal or adapt

Ranked. Mapped to his offer.

1. **Fit call.** Keep it, and keep it first. Nate's motion has no conversation until someone has joined, and even then he writes that he rarely answers in the free group. An owner of a 100-person dealer will not introduce the shop in a 475k room. The fit call is the whole difference.
2. **Job map.** Two sentences on the certification page are how a map should read, pointed at the owner rather than at a student: "Map how the work actually flows today, including the parts nobody documents." And "Define the outcome the client is buying, stated in their numbers rather than your activities." Wil's $217K on 11,139 PO lines is that second sentence already. The map stays a paid, credited product. Theirs is a module that opens inside a cohort.
3. **Build.** Phase 1 ends in "A signed statement of work." That is the right artifact between the map and the build. Wil can hand the owner a scope with a done test. He should not hand them a graded portfolio or a 24-month credential.
4. **Autonomy ladder.** Plus gates "first client" at 30 days and "scale" at 90 days, then a weekly Q&A. That is a course sequence. Wil's ladder is the same job getting more autonomous after the build, with a person on the exceptions. Do not rename the ladder as levels, and do not lock step 3 of a client's own operation behind a membership month.
5. **Proof line.** One named result, and the caveat in the footer of the same page. Their caveat is the part worth copying. Their $5,000–$20,000 and $10K–$25K ranges are not, because the page itself says the ranges are the market.

## What not to copy, and why

- **The free community and the YouTube-to-Skool path.** "Regardless of your background" and "475.3k members" recruit people who want a new income. Wil's buyers already run a company. A public room would spend his week on strangers and still not produce a fit call.
- **AIS+ at $129/month.** The product is "teaching you how to do it too," with client contracts of "$5,000 - $20,000 per month" as the dream, warm outreach and Upwork as the method, and monthly fees non-refundable. Wil is not training the next agency. Publishing $129 next to his offer would also name the wrong unit: a membership, not a job.
- **Two things called certification.** The free challenge says finish and "earn your AIS certification." The paid program is "Certified AI Consultant," "CAIC," "24 months," a letter, a partner listing, and "AIS takes the call." An owner cannot tell those apart from the headlines. Wil should not mint a badge for the work.
- **Scarcity and a locked founder.** "Limited to 50 seats," "Price" is not even on the cohort page, and the Q&A is a lock icon until you pay. Wil's call is free and on his calendar. Hiding it is the wrong direction for a local practice that lives or dies on the first conversation.
- **Gamified access.** Level 3 unlocks chat and the AI OS module for "1% of members." That is a retention machine for a content business. It has nothing to do with handing a dealer their busywork back.
- **Their buyer.** Legal, finance, and procurement at "growing companies," and a network for subcontracting. Noted only as the room he is building. It is not the Idaho and eastern Washington owner.
- **The income table as a price list.** "$1K-$5K one-off projects" versus "$10K-$25K/mo retainers," plus "$200K+ in-house salaries," under a disclosure that these are not graduate results. Using that table to set Wil's build or his ladder would copy a student brochure.
