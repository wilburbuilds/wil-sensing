VI / QUESTIONS

HOW MUCH DOES TEAM TRAINING COST?

It is scoped to your team, so pricing depends on headcount, format, and depth. A free fifteen minute call gives you a firm number and a plan. Most engagements run as a fixed program rather than an hourly rate, so you know the full cost before we start.

WHO ARE THE BOOTCAMPS FOR?

Whole teams, not just engineers. Technical and non-technical tracks run in one shared workspace, so operations, marketing, and finance learn to delegate real tasks to Claude Code and Codex alongside the builders.

DO PEOPLE NEED TO KNOW HOW TO CODE?

No. Most of the people we train have never written code. The tracks meet each group where they work, and everyone ships something real early in the program.

WHAT DOES THE PROGRAM COVER?

Four phases: foundations builds the operating-system mental model and a first win, fluency ships tasks from your actual backlog, workflows wires the tools into your existing processes, and autonomy covers delegation, review, and guardrails. The topic menu on this page is all fair game; we assemble the curriculum that fits your team.

CAN THE TRAINING BE CUSTOMIZED TO OUR STACK AND CODEBASE?

Yes, and that is the point. There is no canned curriculum. Tasks come from your actual backlog, the tools get wired into the workflows your team already runs, and examples use your stack so the skills transfer the moment the session ends.

HOW LONG DOES IT TAKE?

It depends on your team and your calendar. Some companies run the arc in a few intensive weeks, others spread it across a quarter. We scope the timeline on the free call and the program ends when shipping is muscle memory, not on a fixed date.

WHO TEACHES THE PROGRAMS?

Mark Kashef, founder of Prompt Advisers and the instructor behind five live O'Reilly courses on Claude Code and agentic workflows. He holds a master's in AI from Queen's University and has advised hundreds of businesses. Your team learns from him directly, not a junior facilitator.

HOW DO WE START?

A free fifteen minute call scopes the right format: executive briefing, team bootcamp, or train the trainer. You leave with a plan either way.
