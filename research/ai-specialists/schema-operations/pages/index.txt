THE THREE PROBLEMS

What's usually in the way

Different businesses, the same three problems. They feed each other, so the order you fix them in matters.

Read the overview
Design

The company still needs you in every deal. Who decides, what a finished job looks like, and what still has to bounce off you before anything moves. If the business stops when you leave the building, start here.

Margin

Busy, but not keeping enough. Priced to win, fuzzy scope, donated work, and your own unpaid hours making it look profitable. More leads won't fix a thin business. They multiply it.

Inputs

You know what to do. You're not doing enough of it. The two or three actions that move the number, counted every week and protected from the meetings and fires that eat the week. Volume on the right input compounds.

APPROACH

What I actually do with owners

I install simple systems — short rules, named owners, a weekly number — that take the business off your desk. So you can get back to the work you're great at: the work that made the company successful in the first place.

Your week, next to the constraint

Not the story. The calendar and the numbers. If collections are the constraint and you're spending Thursday on brand ideas, there's no mystery.

The few jobs only you should keep

Usually some mix of capital, key relationships, offer design, culture standards and hiring the next layer. Not all of them, and not all at the same depth.

Out of the work that flatters you

The work you're good at and should stop doing is the most addictive work in the company. It also keeps the company the size of one person's arms.

A cadence that doesn't need hustle

Weekly numbers. Clear owners. A short list of priorities that survive Monday. If the company can't run for two weeks without you, that's a design problem.
