Busy is not the same as keeping money

The second pillar, MARGIN. You can be growing and still going broke in slow motion. Most of the time the money is already in the room: in how you price, how you scope, and which work you accept.

Written by
Martin Toomey
Published
20 June 2026
Reading time
8 min read

Share on X
All writing

What this is about

Alot of owners come in asking for leads.

The week is full. The crew is tired. The calendar looks successful.

Then we look at what actually stays in the company after the work is done.

That gap is MARGIN.

Not a slogan. The space between what you bill and what you get to keep after the real cost of doing the job.

You can be growing and still going broke in slow motion. Happens more than people admit.

This is the second pillar. (The overview is in The second stage doesn't start until you fix these three things.)

DESIGN is who decides.
INPUTS is whether you do enough of the right work.
MARGIN is whether the work you already have is worth doing.
The simple version

Imagine a bucket.

You pour water in the top. That is sales.

There are holes in the side. Discounting. Extra scope you did not charge. Rework. Jobs that use your best people for too long. Prices you set three years ago.

You can pour faster. The bucket does not get fuller if the holes are big.

Most "we need marketing" conversations are a person staring at a leaky bucket asking for a bigger hose.

How you got here

Early on, you said yes. You should have. Volume taught you the work.

Then you kept the same yes at a bigger size.

A job that was fine at a smaller scale becomes a quiet tax at $8M or $15M. One extra site visit. One "we will just include that." One senior person babysitting a cheap job.

None of it looks like a crisis on Tuesday. It shows up in what you take home. Or what you cannot take home.

You feel busy. So you assume the answer is more top of funnel.

Sometimes you do need more. A lot of the time the money is already in the room. Hiding in how you price, how you scope, and which work you accept.

What MARGIN actually means here

I am not talking about a perfect spreadsheet that nobody updates.

I mean a clear view of:

What a job really costs.
People time.
Rework.
The owner's hours you pretend are free.
What you charge.
And what you give away after the quote.
Which work looks good and pays bad.
Which work pays and you keep underpricing because you are scared to lose it.
Which offers should die.

You do not need forty reports. You need enough truth to stop feeding the wrong jobs.

The lead trap

I hear this a lot.

"Get us more conversations."

Then we look at close rates that are fine and jobs that eat the profit after the celebration email.

More of that is not a strategy. It is a louder version of the same week.

Nadia, Owen and Kim below are composites, drawn from more than one company, with names and details changed.

A client, Nadia, came in hot on ads. Schedule was full. She was not keeping what the top line suggested.

We did not touch campaigns first. We sat with completed jobs. The "easy" accounts that always needed one more run. The rush fees she waived. The SKUs that tied up the floor and paid like leftovers.

Ugly conversations. A couple of long-time customers did not love the new floor. She held it anyway.

They were around $9M and tired. A year later closer to $13M, and the take-home finally looked like a company that size. Not because the market got kinder. Because she stopped donating margin.

Where the money leaks

A short list. You can probably circle two without thinking.

Price that never moved while costs did.
Scope that grows after the handshake and never hits a change order.
Your best people on the cheapest work because they are "safe."
The owner doing delivery for free and calling it quality.
Discounts to win a logo you will brag about and regret.
Saying yes to ugly work to keep the crew busy. Busy crew on bad work is still a loss.
Rework you do not track because it would make you angry.

None of this is clever. It is just ignored because looking at it is less fun than a new funnel.

What we do first

We pick a slice of work. Last quarter. Real jobs. Not the brochure jobs.

We ask what you billed. What it took. Who touched it. How many times it came back.

Then we sort.

Keep and raise.
Keep and tighten.
Raise or kill.
Kill.

Owners fight the kill pile. I get it. That pile is often a friend, or an early customer, or a type of job you used to need.

If it taxes the team and pays like a hobby, it is not loyalty. It is a leak with a name.

Another client, Owen, ran a dental group. Full books. Thin keep. The schedule looked like success.

We found the money in how they packed the day and what they discounted to "fill gaps." Gaps were fake. They were just cheap slots.

We changed the mix. Fewer giveaways. Clearer packages. The floor stopped being a suggestion.

They went from about $11M with almost no breathing room to roughly $15M with a week that did not feel like a grind every Friday. Still a practice. Just not a charity with drills.

Harsh truth

If you are scared to raise the floor, say that. Do not dress it up as "the market."

Sometimes the market is tight. A lot of the time you trained the market to wait you out.

You were the one who said yes to the extra. You were the one who flinched on price. The team copied you. Design and margin live next to each other here. If every discount still needs your nod, you will nod. You are tired. Tired owners discount.

Give someone else a floor and a spine. Then stop undercutting them.

What this is not

This is not "charge more and be rude." It is charge for the job you actually do.

This is not cutting people first. Labor is not always the hole. The hole is often the work you ask labor to do.

This is not a 60-tab model. If the model needs a priest, you will not use it.

This is not ads. Ads come after the bucket holds water. Or they come in parallel if the input problem is real. They do not come first just because you are uncomfortable looking at jobs.

How you know this is your pillar
Revenue is up or flat-high and you still feel poor.
You would not want ten more of your average job.
You win work and feel a little sick.
Your best weeks are the ones where a job slips, not the ones where you sell more.
You cannot explain which offer prints money.
You want a marketing hire and you cannot say what a good job is worth.

If that is you, more conversations will not save the week.

A third pass

Client Kim in logistics. Quotes were winning. Drivers were maxed. She thought the answer was another salesperson.

We priced the ugly lanes honestly for the first time. Some work was a trophy. Trophy work that loses money is still a loss.

She dropped two accounts that made the office cheer and the bank account shrug. Raised two others. Tightened when "just this once" could happen.

From about $14M stuck-flat to a bit over $18M. Not a miracle year. A cleaner book of work.

She was short with me in month one. Fine. The P&L was shorter with her before that.

How DESIGN and INPUTS sneak in

If every price exception comes back to you, margin dies in your texts at 9pm. That is design.

If you know the good work and you spend the week on the other work, that is inputs.

Do not mix the words to avoid the math. Pick the hole. Fix that hole.

What to change in thirty days
Pick twenty recent jobs. Real ones.
Write billed. Write a rough true cost. Include owner time as if you paid a person.
Sort the four piles: keep and raise, keep and tighten, raise or kill, kill.
Change one rule this month. Floor. Change order. Which jobs you refuse.
Tell the team the rule in a short meeting. Not a novel.

Watch one cycle of work.

If you flinch, notice it. Flinching is data.

What it feels like when it works

The calendar can look a little lighter and the bank account a little heavier.

That confuses people who only watch top line.

You stop celebrating bad wins.

The team stops treating extra work as a personality trait.

You can buy attention later if you still need it. You are not using ads to hide a leak.

If this is the problem

Do not send me a brand deck.

Send the shape of the week and what you think a good job pays.

If you are busy and not keeping enough, send me a note with the word MARGIN.
