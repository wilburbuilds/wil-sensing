Lean teams survive and thrive

When the next downturn comes, lean teams that own the yes move fast and protect margin. Companies where every decision waits for the founder run out of cash before the cut is approved.

Written by
Martin Toomey
Published
19 October 2026
Reading time
3 min read

Share on X
All writing

Lean teams that own the yes survive and thrive. Businesses that don't will close.

With insecurity both in the US and globally increasing, the likelihood of another major downturn becomes more probable.

In 2001 the lowest one-year survival rates for new establishments were for businesses opened that year. From 2008 to 2010 the country lost more than 170,000 small businesses with fewer than 100 employees. Very small shops exited at roughly twice the rate of larger ones. In early 2020 roughly 40 percent of small businesses temporarily closed and average employment fell nearly 40 percent. The ones that stayed open had already reduced to the work that cleared cash and had people who could execute the cut without waiting.

A lean team that owns the yes runs faster and protects margin because the small decisions no longer sit in a queue. When every pricing exception, scope change, or product keep/kill still requires the founder, the company runs at the speed of one person's calendar. Leads wait. Low-margin work keeps shipping. Hours disappear into status checks and rewrites. When volume drops, those delays compound and cash leaves before the cut gets approved. Founder-led companies between $2M and $15M are usually built this way. Every real decision still comes through the owner. Cutting headcount without first writing who owns the outcome just creates more messages. Tightening a product line without a written rule leaves the team protecting the old mix because "we'll figure it out" still means "ask the owner."

The system starts with the growth chart. Pull the five numbers that actually move cash and volume. Assign an owner to each. The KEEP / GIVE / KILL list sits next to it so product lines have the same rule. The remaining people are narrower on purpose. Each one named to a number or an outcome. When volume drops, the person who owns the line already knows what stays. Cycle time shortens. Labor hours that used to be spent waiting disappear. Margin stays higher because the work that does not clear cash after direct cost is already named and already killable. Fewer people produce more usable output per hour because they are not messengers.

The founder's calendar clears. Time that used to go to every exception now goes to the few decisions that expand the business. The scorecard meeting still runs. The team still moves the numbers. The company can get smaller without stalling because the system no longer depends on one person being available for every yes.

My Design System installs that structure. One function at a time. Scorecard from the growth chart, named owners, and a written KEEP / GIVE / KILL on the lines that actually contribute. When the next cut comes, the team already has the rule and the right to use it.

If the map of which lines pay and who owns the numbers is still only in your head, that is the starting point. The install builds it on your actual company so the lean version does not require you on every thread.
