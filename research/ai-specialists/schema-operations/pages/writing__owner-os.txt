Owner OS

A company that cannot run without you is not a company. Seven moves to take the business off the founder: foundation, vision, perception, team, the 20%, paying the owner, and exiting the job.

Written by
Martin Toomey
Published
24 November 2025
Reading time
4 min read

0. The rule

Acompany that cannot run without you is not a company.

It is a job with extra steps, extra staff, and a story about "being in the weeds."

Design. Margins. Inputs.

Miss one and the industry is not the problem.

1. Foundation: how you became the machine

You did not fail. You adapted.

Early on, you had to be the system. Sales, quality, cash, firefighting. That built the thing. Then the thing grew and the habit stayed.

Foundation is not journaling. It is naming the cost:

Every decision still hits your phone.
Staff wait instead of own.
You call it standards. The P&L calls it a bottleneck.

Write one sentence:

"The business needs me for X. It uses me for everything else."

If you cannot split those two lists, you do not have a company yet.

2. Vision: the company after you leave the chair

Not a vision board. A working picture.

One sentence:

"In 12 months I am not required for daily revenue, daily quality, or daily cash."

If that sentence scares you, good. That fear is why design never gets finished.

Crystal clear means you can answer:

What still requires me?
What only feels like it requires me?
What breaks if I disappear for 14 days?

If the answer is "everything," the vision is still "keep me indispensable."

3. Perception: they still think this is you Inc.

Clients buy you. Staff wait on you. Vendors ask for you. Lenders underwrite you.

That is not brand. That is concentration risk.

Perception work is not personal branding. It is transferring trust off your name and onto roles, process, and proof.

Who is the face of delivery?
Who owns the number when you are silent?
What artifact proves the company exists without your calendar?

Until perception moves, you can hire people and still be the product.

4. Team: ownership or expensive company

Headcount without scorecards is a coping mechanism.

A dream team in this system is not loyalty. It is named ownership.

For the top three seats:

One number they own.
One decision they can make without you.
One weekly scorecard you read instead of a story.

If they cannot fail on a number, they do not own anything. You do.

Do not hire to feel less lonely in the 80%. Hire to take the 20% you should not be touching.

5. Action plan: the 20% only

The plan is not more hours. It is fewer inputs.

List the work that actually produces cash, margin, or a company that can run. That is the 20%.

Everything else is hiding with a to-do list.

Owner OS action plan for 30 days:

Pick the constraint: design, margins, or inputs. One.
Cut one offer, client type, or meeting that does not pay.
Move founder hours onto the 20% calendar only.
Install three scorecards.
Run a no-rescue rule. If they own it, you do not take it back.

Volume on the right input. Perfection is not available. The yes takes care of itself after the calls, the conversations, and the follow-up exist.

6. Pay the owner: margin is a design choice

If the company cannot pay you first through a normal month, you are funding a hobby that employs people.

"Good enough at a profit" is the standard. Unpaid internships with your name on the door are not.

Rules:

Price for the business, not for your guilt.
Stop work that only looks premium.
Owner pay is a line item, not leftover hope.
If volume rises and cash does not, you do not have growth. You have a bigger job.

Pay yourself so the company has to become a company.

7. Exit the job, not necessarily the business

Exit strategy here means you leave the operator seat on a date.

Set it. Treat it like a close.

14-day absence test, then 30.
Roles that do not route through you.
Sales that does not need your charisma on every call.
Cash and quality that survive your silence.

Selling the company is optional. Selling yourself back your life is the product.

When you no longer need the business to prove you are useful, the business can finally be useful.

Which one is broken?

Most founders do not have a market problem. They have a weak company and a long explanation.
