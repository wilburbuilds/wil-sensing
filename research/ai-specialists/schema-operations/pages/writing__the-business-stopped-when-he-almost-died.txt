The business stopped when he almost died

He ate at gas stations, skipped the gym, and every decision still came to him. Then he had a heart attack, and the business stopped with him.

Written by
Martin Toomey
Published
12 March 2026
Reading time
2 min read

Share on X
All writing

The body does not send a clean invoice. It just stops covering the decisions you have not moved.

He ate most of his meals at gas stations. Breakfast was whatever was hot under the lamp. Lunch was a sandwich and a bag of chips between job sites. Dinner was whatever he could grab on the way home or skip entirely. He had not trained in over a year. He told himself the company needed him more than the gym did. The company agreed, because every pricing exception, every schedule conflict, and every customer who wanted "the owner" still came to him.

He had a heart attack on a Tuesday afternoon. He was in the hospital for six days and home for another three weeks with strict limits on what he could do. The business did not have a version of itself that could run without him. Change orders sat. Invoices past a certain size were not sent. Two key customers called and were told someone would get back to them. No one did, because the person who normally made those calls was not allowed to work and no one else had the written right to make them. Cash stopped moving. Payroll was covered by the line of credit. By the time he could concentrate enough to look at the business, the company was three weeks from missing a major payment and two customers had already started talking to competitors.

He almost went bankrupt because the business literally stopped while he was in the hospital recovering.

We started with the decisions that had frozen. We wrote the three things a project manager could approve on a change order without him, the dollar amount on invoices that no longer needed his sign-off, and the two customer situations that still required his yes once he was back. The rest had a named owner and a clear boundary. Cash started moving again within ten days. The customers who had been waiting got answers from someone who could actually give them. He came back to a company that was no longer one hospital stay away from insolvency.

He is fixing himself now. The gas station meals are mostly gone. He is walking every day and back in the gym three mornings a week. The business no longer requires the version of him that was eating in the truck and skipping recovery. The decisions that used to pull him out of that recovery now have owners. He still makes the calls that actually need him. He is no longer the only reason the company can function when he cannot.
