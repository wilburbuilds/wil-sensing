You already know what to do

The third pillar, INPUTS. You know the two or three actions that move the number. You're not doing enough of them. Volume on the right input is what compounds.

Written by
Martin Toomey
Published
27 June 2026
Reading time
7 min read

Share on X
All writing

What this is about

This is the third of three pillars. The first is DESIGN: who decides. The second is MARGIN: whether the work pays. This one is INPUTS: whether the right work happens in enough volume to matter. (The overview is in The second stage doesn't start until you fix these three things.)

It's the one owners argue with most, because it sounds like a lecture about discipline. It is not that.

You should know the two or three actions that move the number the most — the 20% of inputs that produce 80% of the results.

You are not doing enough of them.

The week is filled with other things. Meetings that feel like work. Slack that feels like leadership. Tiny fires that feel urgent because they are loud. Meetings that should have been emails, emails that should have been a text, and texts that shouldn't have been sent.

Thinking is cheap. A clean plan in a notebook does not book a job.

The simple version

A pump.

You can own a great pump. You may know it should run four hours a day. If it runs twenty minutes, you do not have a pump problem. You have a running problem.

Founders love a new pump. New CRM. New script. New content plan. New offer name.

The old pump was fine. Nobody turned it on long enough.

How the week dies

The morning block was supposed to be outbound. Or reviews. Or hiring screens. Or whatever your real input is.

Then twenty small asks land. You are still the easy button.

Sometimes DESIGN failed, so INPUTS starve. Sometimes the work pays badly, so you chase random jobs: MARGIN failed, so INPUTS point at the wrong target. And sometimes the design and the margin are good enough and you still hide in tasks that feel skilled.

What an input is

An input is a thing you can count that sits before the result.

Not "grow." Not "be more strategic." Not "get the brand out there."

Examples:

The number of first conversations with the right buyer.
The number of quotes that match the good work, not the junk work.
The number of jobs reviewed against the rule.
The number of hours the lead spends selling instead of babysitting.
The number of times you protect the morning block and actually use it.

If you cannot count it, it is a wish.

Wishes do not compound. Volume on the right input does.

The plan addiction

New plans feel like progress. Your brain gets a prize.

Doing Thursday's calls does not feel like a prize. It feels like work. So you redesign the deck.

I have sat with owners who had four strategies in a year and the same empty outreach number every month.

Three stories from the field

These are composites, drawn from more than one company, with names and details changed.

Luis, in home services, knew his growth sat in a simple loop. Visit the right neighborhoods. Follow up the same week. Ask for the referral while the job was still warm. He had a binder. He did not have a week.

We killed two meetings and put the loop on the board with a number.

They were around $3.5M. The next year, a bit over $5M. Same trucks. More of the action he already believed in. He did the things that matter more often.

Fiona runs a training company. Smart. Too smart for her own week. She kept building the next product, when the current product already sold whenever she talked to people.

Talking to people was the input. Building was the hiding place. So we set a minimum number of conversations each week, and product work only happened once she'd hit it.

She thought it was crude. Ninety days later the pipeline looked like a company again: about $7M to a little over $10M. She still builds. After the count, not instead of it.

Hasan is in industrial supply. He knew the number moved when he was in front of mid-size plants, not the tiny accounts that called all day. The tiny accounts were loud. The plants were quiet and slow.

We boxed the small stuff into a team with rules. His week got three plant days. He felt rude. He was not rude. He was finally making progress.

$4M-ish and stuck. Then toward $6M, without a new identity. Same man. Different calendar.

What we actually do

Name the one or two inputs. Not ten. Ten is how you hide.

Put them on a week view, not a quarterly vision board.

Cut what steals the time block. Real cuts. Recurring meetings that exist because nobody killed them.

If the founder is the only one who can do the input, that is a design problem wearing an INPUTS mask. Fix who can make the call. Then raise the count.

If the input is "more of the bad job," that is a margin problem. Do not grind harder on a leak.

Then we watch four weeks. Not one heroic Monday.

Heroic Mondays lie.

Hard truth

You do not need another framework.

You need to stop treating the important work as something you'll get to when things calm down. In a business, things never calm down.

Protect the time block like you would protect payroll, because it is how payroll stays possible. If you cannot protect two hours, we do not talk about scale. We talk about the two hours.

How you know this is you
You can explain the strategy in your sleep.
The scoreboard on activity is thin.
You have tools you barely open.
You say "I just need to get to it" every month.
You feel busy and cannot point to the actions that create next quarter.
You hire thinkers when you needed more of the same simple motion.

If that's the mirror, read to the end before you do anything about it.

Inputs and the other two pillars

People want one word for a mixed mess.

If you cannot increase the inputs because nobody else can make a decision, that is DESIGN. Go there first.

If you are pounding volume into work that does not pay, that is MARGIN. More input makes a bigger mess.

If both are good enough and the count is still low, stay here. Do not hide in a reorg.

What good looks like

A week you could explain to a twelve-year-old:

"We talk to this many of the right people."
"We quote this many of the good jobs."
"We review this many completed jobs against the rule."

A board or a sheet with those numbers. Paper taped to a wall is fine. Ugly is fine.

A founder who uses the morning to do the 20%.

A team that knows the count matters more than the mood in the group chat.

Thirty days
Write the result you want in one line. Revenue is allowed. So is "I am not on every call."
Write the one action that most leads to that result.
Write last week's true count. Not the story. The count.
Set a number that is a stretch and not a fantasy.
Kill one standing meeting. Put the action where the meeting was.
Tell one person to interrupt you only if the building is actually on fire.
Review the count every Friday, in fifteen minutes. No slides.
When it starts working

The week feels plainer. That scares some owners. The ceremony felt like importance.

Plain weeks print money.

You will still miss days. Fine. Four weeks of half-decent volume beats one intense week and a collapse.

The company stops waiting for a new idea and starts waiting for Friday's number.

If this is your problem

You know what to do. You are not doing enough of it.

That sentence stings. It is also a relief. You do not need a shiny new tool. You need a week that matches what you already believe.

If that is the sticking point, send me a note with the word INPUTS.
