You trained the new hire on the software. You never trained them on the decisions.

The onboarding covered the tools. It never covered which outcomes the new person owns, so six months later the same questions still reach you.

Written by
Martin Toomey
Published
24 April 2026
Reading time
2 min read

Share on X
All writing

The new person knows how to use the tools. They can update the CRM, generate the quote draft, log the job, and send the status email. Six months later the same questions still reach you. The onboarding covered the software. It did not cover which outcomes they own and which situations still require someone else.

Most owners treat the hire as a capacity add and the software as the system. They walk the person through logins, fields, and workflows. They show them where completed work goes. They spend little time on the actual decisions the role is supposed to finish without a thread. The result is a competent user of the tools who has learned that anything non-standard, or anything that feels like it might matter, comes back for review. The software makes the handoff clean. It does not make the handoff unnecessary.

One client hired an estimator. The first two weeks were spent on the quoting software, the CRM, and the shared folders. The estimator could produce a complete first draft on standard work within 3 days. Any job that involved a custom material, a deadline inside ten days, or a customer who had changed scope on a prior order was still sent to the owner with a note. The estimator was not slow. He had never been given the written line on those three cases or told that he owned the outcome inside it. We wrote the specific conditions he could quote and schedule on his own, the two situations that still needed a ten-minute review, and what "done" looked like so the job could move without a second edit. He kept using the same software. The number of drafts that reached the owner dropped because the decisions, not the tool training, had been clarified.

Tool proficiency and decision ownership are separate. A person can be fast inside the system and still treat every judgment call as above their role. The company then pays for the hire, pays for the software, and continues to pay with the founder's time on items the hire could have closed. The fix is not more software training. It is stating, before or immediately after the person starts, the outcomes they own, the boundaries on those outcomes, and the short list of cases that still come back. Once that exists, the tools they were already shown become useful for work they can actually finish. Until it exists, the tools mainly help them prepare better packets for the same inbox.
