Your best customers will leave if you get hit by a bus

The CRM is current and the team can do the work. But the customers and referral partners who matter still call the founder, so the accounts that count depend on one person's phone.

Written by
Martin Toomey
Published
2 April 2026
Reading time
2 min read

Share on X
All writing

The company has a salesperson and project managers. The CRM is current. The jobs still get done. A meaningful share of the revenue and the problem-solving continues to depend on relationships that only exist with the founder. Customers and referral sources know the company name. They call the owner when something matters.

The pattern is quiet. The team can handle the routine work. The moment a job is late, a price is questioned, or a scope needs interpretation, the customer asks for the person they already trust. The founder takes the call because refusing it risks the relationship. The team learns that sensitive issues get routed upward to protect the account. Over time the founder remains the only one with real standing, even though other people are capable of the work.

One owner ran a $6.9 million specialty contracting company. He had two project managers and a full pipeline. Three of his largest customers still called him directly when a change order exceeded a few thousand dollars or when a schedule slipped. They had his cell number from the early years. The project managers could explain the facts. The customers wanted the person who had originally earned their trust. He spent several hours a week on calls that restated information the team already had. When he was unreachable for four days, two of those customers paused decisions until he returned. The work did not stop. The decisions that affected cash did.

Another owner had grown a $4.7 million services firm largely through referrals. The referral partners sent work to him by name. His team ran the projects once they started. When a referral partner had a question about fit or timing, they called him. He had never introduced the partners to anyone else with the authority to answer. The pipeline looked healthy in the CRM. A portion of it still depended on his availability and his personal reputation. He could not point to a single referral relationship that would continue at the same level if he stepped back for a month.

The team is not incapable. They have simply never been given the combination of information, authority, and introduction that would let the customer treat them as a real alternative. The founder keeps the relationships because transferring them feels riskier than keeping them. The company stays dependent on one person's phone and reputation for the accounts that matter most.

We start by identifying the customers and referral sources who still default to the founder. We write the specific situations they currently escalate, the information the team needs to handle them, and the boundary on what still requires the founder. Then the founder makes the introduction and states the new path in front of the customer. The relationship does not disappear. It gains a second person who can act on it. The founder keeps the accounts that truly need his judgment. He stops being the only reason those accounts continue to function when he is not available.
