# What Wil Sensing should take from the AI specialists

Prepared for Wil Sensing. Sources fetched 26 October 2026. Read-only. No forms submitted, no calls booked, no signups.

This note sits on top of the company teardowns in `research/ai-specialists/`. Every price and quote below was checked against a saved page or against `research/ai-specialists/_x/posts.md`. The company file is named when the quote lives there. **INFERENCE** marks a conclusion that is not a sentence on any of those surfaces. Private-equity motions are labeled "Noted for reference, NOT our strategy for now."

Wil Sensing is a one-person consultancy for local and mid-sized businesses (about 50 to 200 people: dealerships, equipment dealers, trades; Idaho and eastern Washington). The offer, in order, is a free 30-minute fit call, a paid Job map credited toward the build, the build, then ongoing work that moves each job up an autonomy ladder (tool adoption, then headless, then governed, then agent-to-agent, with a person on the exceptions). The line is "Hand off the busywork. Keep the decisions." The proof on hand is a local auto repair shop: $217K in missed vendor credits found by matching 11,139 PO lines. Pricing is undecided.

The Limestone Digital teardown is the format these notes follow. It is not repeated here.

## Top 10 takeaways

1. **The Job map is the product they describe and do not sell.** Helion's seven-part plan names the problem, the measure, the owner, the data, who connects it, the access, and a pilot that ends in go or no-go. "A license is a tool. A plan is a decision." https://heliontechnologies.com/dealership-ai-implementation-plan/ Varick sorts each step into code, an agent, or a person. Building AI Agents writes "the baseline, target, and how it will be tested" before work starts. https://www.buildingaiagents.ai/agency-site?v=20260723-26 Pixel's Define step agrees "what we're doing, what it costs, how long it should take, and what success looks like." https://pixelnarratives.studio/implementation None of them say the buyer pays for that page and gets the fee back off the build. That sentence is Wil's.

2. **Keep the fit call at 30 minutes, and let it end in a no.** Schema says "I'll tell you if it's a fit." https://schemaoperations.com/services/ Boom says "If we are not the right fit, we will say so." https://boomautomations.com/ Mad City says "We will tell you if a first build is even a good idea." https://www.madcityintegrations.com/calculator Prompt Advisers promises a straight no, then scopes the paid work on a fifteen-minute call. https://www.promptadvisers.com/consulting Steal the no. Leave the fifteen minutes and the price on the same call. The map is the next meeting, and it is paid.

3. **Do not copy a free prototype week.** Tenex's X post is a free 5-day bootcamp with prototypes on the buyer's data. The site's diagnostic is a complimentary two weeks, and another page sells 30-, 60-, or 90-day audits. They are not one product, and none of them is a credited Job map. https://x.com/businessbarista/status/2105404947106075038 and https://www.tenex.co/diagnostic Boom's playbook, which is the agency-coaching business, describes a $3K audit credited toward the build. That credit shape is his. The playbook's buyer is a person starting an AI agency. Leave that buyer.

4. **Write the agent the way Helion writes it, on the ladder.** "An agent should never run as a person. Not as the IT manager. Not as the controller. Never as an administrator." It gets its own account, the least access the job needs, a log, and one named owner. A recon agent "doesn't move anything. It doesn't reprice anything. It reads. It reports." https://heliontechnologies.com/ai-agents-for-car-dealerships/ That is tool, then headless, then governed, with a person on the exception. It is also his headline. He does not need a help desk or a per-user MSP tier to say it.

5. **Put the client's own system in the sentence with the number, and say what the number is not.** Sustain Media's Portland block says what "their ServiceTitan shows," not "we generated." https://www.sustain-media.com/ The playbook's fencing story leads with $934,685 and then says it is total company sales, not all of it from the work. https://www.sustain-media.com/full-home-service-seo-playbook.html Varick's finance study shows the dollar math and the items left out. Wil's $217K and 11,139 lines should be written that way: what "missed vendor credits" includes, and that a person still checks the exception. Do not borrow Sustain's ads retainer, Varick's millions, or Boom's "95+" against "90+" against "80+."

6. **One job, in the buyer's nouns.** Frontier's HVAC page is "Techs call you from the attic." https://frontierbusinessdesign.com/hvac/ Agency VA names certificates, endorsements, and policy checking, then says on X that the agency has a manual-work problem, not a staffing problem. https://x.com/TF27wess/status/2105281521523585191 Hello Paralegal's useful post walks one intake through one evening. The homepage sells paralegals from $5 an hour. Wil's page should do the post, not the catalog: PO lines, vendor credits, the DMS, the person who still approves.

7. **Their published prices are not his, and several of them disagree with themselves.** The landscape is below. The pattern is that a floor without a unit, or two floors for the same tier, is worse than no number. DJV's English Tier Two is "From USD 8,000" and the Spanish page is "Desde USD 5,000." https://consultingdjv.com/ and https://consultingdjv.com/es Boom's 90-day guarantee is "measurable results" and the page never says what is measured. https://boomautomations.com/ If Wil publishes a number, publish it for the Job map, say whether it comes off the build, and leave their floors alone.

8. **Ongoing work is a rung the owner can stop, not a seat.** Frontier's System Build is "one defined project… handed off working," from $2,500. The Operating Partner is "I embed as your part-time COO," from $3,000 a month. https://frontierbusinessdesign.com/packages/ Schema's $12,000 month has a six-month minimum. Pixel's Enterprise Partner starts at $15,000 a month. Prompt Advisers' implementation card is "$50K AND UP." Wil stays for the next rung of the same job. He does not take the COO seat, the MSP seat, or the enterprise floor.

9. **The guru funnels sell a different buyer.** LeftClick's agency band is $5,000 to start and most projects $10,000 to $50,000. Maker School is $184 a month for people who want their first AI client. https://leftclick.ai/pricing.html and https://www.skool.com/makerschool/about Nate Herk's public group is free. Plus is $129 a month and opens "get your first client." https://www.skool.com/ai-automation-society-plus/about Boom's founder site and 180-day plan are the same motion: coaching for people building agencies. Wil's buyer already owns the shop. Do not build the community, the cohort, or the Inner Circle.

10. **Proof that is a calculator, a fiction, or a headcount is the pattern to leave.** Mad City's $2,946 is a calculator default, and Willow & Wok is "a fictional restaurant." https://www.madcityintegrations.com/calculator The $1,000 setup and $250 a month are the X post about a first client, and the post says the system still has to be proved. Boom's homepage percents are not attached to a named person. Prompt Advisers' homepage proof is subscribers, courses, and "500+ businesses advised," and `/case-studies` is a 404. Wil already has the stronger owner number, if the shop will let him use it and if he writes the caveat.

## What Wil should steal or adapt

Ranked. Each item maps to the fit call, the credited Job map, the build, or the ladder.

1. **Put Helion's seven rows inside the paid Job map.** Problem, today's number, the named owner, the systems and fields, who connects them, the access, and a pilot that ends in go or no-go. His measure row can already say $217K and 11,139 PO lines, and what a person still checks. Do not give the page away as a free assessment. https://heliontechnologies.com/dealership-ai-implementation-plan/

2. **Say the credit out loud.** Pixel's Explore starts at $1,250 and does not say the fee comes off a later build. Boom's $1,250 hour is credited toward Boom or the Inner Circle, which is the coaching product. Schema's $6,000 install is a different door from the $397 kit, not a credit. Wil's difference is one sentence: the Job map fee comes off the build. Add it. Do not adopt their dollars.

3. **Let the 30 minutes end in a no, and start from the job.** Bring the PO lines or the credit list, the way Mad City says "bring last month's leads." Schema's qualifying sentence is the right length. The form stays short: the job, a way to reply, and no revenue wizard. Prompt Advisers' fifteen-minute scope-on-the-spot is the version to leave, because it tries to be the map and the price.

4. **Interview the person who does the work. Do not build on their data for free.** Tenex's useful line is "walk me through the exact steps. How many hours a week? Who else touches it? What breaks?" That belongs in the fit call and then in the map. The free prototypes on real data stay theirs. https://www.tenex.co/playbooks/build-an-ai-strategy-that-works

5. **Sort the job into code, an agent, and a person, and write what each may do.** Varick's "Don't Apply AI" is the sort. Helion's table is the rules: what the agent may do alone, what a person signs, and what it never does. The recon example is the first rung: read and report, do not reprice, do not move the record. That is the ladder under "Hand off the busywork. Keep the decisions."

6. **Agree the unit before the build.** Building AI Agents documents baseline, target, and the test, in hours, response time, errors, or revenue waiting. Pixel agrees the work, the price, the time, and what success looks like before anyone builds. Wil's unit can be credits found, lines matched, and the exception a person still handles. Write it in the map. Do not print Boom's "measurable results" or Prompt Advisers' "until it works" without that unit.

7. **One job, then the next rung, stoppable.** Hello Paralegal's useful order is one leak, let it run, then the next. Frontier's test is "Built ≠ Owned": a binder nobody follows is not done. Wil's ongoing work is the next rung of that job, not Schema's six-month seat and not Frontier's part-time COO.

8. **Write the shop story in Sustain's shape and Varick's caveat.** Lead with the job and the number. Name the system the number came from. Say what the number is not. Say the person who still decides. One story. Not a grid of unnamed percents, and not a calculator with sample inputs.

9. **Use the buyer's nouns, and only for work he has done.** A dealer page can sound like Helion's service drive. A trade page can sound like Frontier's attic. An insurance page is Agency VA's, and it is not his market. The habit is the screen and the task. The catalog is theirs.

10. **Say where the work happens.** Helion: its own account, read-only first, no admin. Varick: in the system they already run, and a person can pause it. Hello Paralegal's post: the client's information stays on the firm's systems. His version is the DMS, QuickBooks, or the vendor portal he already used for the PO match. He does not paste the customer file into a public model.

## What not to copy

- **Their prices as his.** The table above is the landscape. A 50-to-200-person dealer is not Prompt Advisers' $50k card, Pixel's $15,000 month, Schema's $12,000 seat, or Helion's per-user MSP. Publishing any of those numbers would price a bench he does not staff.
- **A guarantee whose unit is missing.** Boom's 90-day "measurable results." Prompt Advisers' "the engagement ends when it works." Sustain's older "top 3 in six months or we work for free," on a page that calls the buyer "your practice." A promise he cannot score becomes the first objection.
- **Free discovery that includes the build.** Tenex's prototypes. Building AI Agents' free consultation that "prices" the work without a number. Helion's no-cost assessment that stacks vendor bills. His discovery is the fit call. The artifact is the paid map.
- **The second business, which is selling to people who want to become him.** Boom's Inner Circle and 180-day plan. LeftClick's Maker School at $184 a month. Nate Herk's Plus at $129 a month, which opens "get your first client." The certification that does not show tuition. That buyer is an agency founder. His buyer owns the shop.
- **Headcount, fictional demos, and a calculator as proof.** Agency VA's 2,100 VAs. Prompt Advisers' subscriber count. Mad City's Willow & Wok. Boom's 95+ against 90+ against 80+, and 48 logo files under a sentence that says 95+. Wil is one person. The shop story is the proof that fits.
- **A staffing offer wearing an AI sentence.** Hello Paralegal's $5 an hour and the 5% recruitment fee. Agency VA's monthly people-prices, with AVA Insurance OS unpriced. The X line about manual work is worth stealing. The bench is not.
- **An ads retainer.** Sustain Media is the best proof shape in the set and the wrong product. $3,500 to $10,000 a month for Maps, SEO, and ads is not a Job map.
- **Private equity as a go-to-market.** Tenex sells bootcamps toward "PE portcos." Varick has a private-equity essay. Noted for reference, NOT our strategy for now. The transferable piece is one measured job, then the same job at the next similar company. That is the next shop, not a fund.
- **Conflicting public facts.** DJV's $8,000 and $5,000 for Tier Two. LeftClick's monthly revenue figures that change by page. Hello Paralegal's $5 an hour, the 5% fee, and the $600–$1,200 blog price, which are three products. Copying their confidence is useful. Copying a number they do not agree on is how a small firm gets caught.
- **A 15-minute call that quotes the build.** Prompt Advisers. And a four-week embed sold as an audit. Varick. Wil's split stays: 30 minutes to see if the job is real, then a paid map, then the build.
