THE PROBLEM
You've got AI. You don't have value.
You've tried the obvious fixes. None of them solved the real problem.
You bought AI tools.
Your team has Copilot licenses. A handful of enthusiasts got faster. The rest kept working the old way. Deployment frequency stayed flat. 80% of AI initiatives never deliver measurable business value.
You hired more engineers.
Headcount went up. Delivery didn't. More people means more coordination, more reviews, more standups. Brooks's Law isn't a theory. It's your last three quarters.
You brought in vendors.
They closed tickets. They didn't own outcomes. Your leads spent more time managing contractors than building product. The code shipped. The value didn't.
Your best engineers are stuck.
Senior devs get 2 hours of deep focus per 8-hour day. The rest is code review, incident response, and context switching. That's exactly the work AI should have absorbed by now.
Nobody can see where the hours go.
Leadership wants faster delivery. Engineering says they're at capacity. Both are right. Getting an answer means stitching together four or five different tools. The drag sits in the pipeline, and nobody's measured it.
The problem isn't your team. It's the invisible friction between your team and production.

Help & Support
Enterprise AI transformation questions, answered straight.
What does enterprise AI transformation actually mean?
We find the bottlenecks that cost you the most, ship AI-driven solutions inside the product you already run, train your team to use them reliably, and measure the uplift weekly. Then we repeat the loop, stage by stage, from AI-absent to AI-native.
How is an enterprise AI transformation partner different from a dev shop?
Dev shops close tickets. We own outcomes: every pod is measured against your own delivery baseline on our Dev Intelligence Platform dashboards, and the engagement is month-to-month. No measured uplift, no invoice.
Are you consultants or engineers?
Engineers who think strategically. They ask what a feature does to your margin before they ask which framework to use. Engagements start with a diagnostic: we map your bottlenecks, baseline your delivery metrics, and agree where AI pays. Then we embed in your existing team and work all three layers: AI-first workflows and training until the habits stick, custom agents on your data, and AI-native delivery in your repo. Shipped software is one layer. The bigger win is a team that keeps building this way without us. You get advice on architecture and build-vs-buy along the way, never a 200-slide deck someone else has to execute.
Will enterprise AI transformation work on a 10-year-old legacy codebase?
That’s most of our work. We’re brownfield first: your existing stack, your constraints, no rewrite. Applied AI pays fastest in older codebases, because that’s where the drag is: tests, migrations, refactors, documentation. Your customers never see a rewrite. They see faster releases.
We already bought Copilot. Why isn't it moving the numbers?
Tools lift individuals; they don’t change how a team ships. A handful of enthusiasts got faster. The rest kept working the old way. We install the operating model around the tools: workflow, review gates, measurement. Uplift shows up in deployment frequency, not autocomplete stats.
How do we trust AI-written code in production?
Every line is reviewed, tested, and traceable to a human owner. Deterministic quality gates run before any merge, and our Dev Intelligence Platform tracks AI-assisted changes separately, so you can audit exactly what shipped. Nothing goes to production on vibes.
Who handles AI governance and compliance?
We build the governance layer in from day one: model and tool choices that fit your regulatory constraints, audit trails on AI-assisted changes, and usage policies your CISO can sign. Work stays in your repos, your cloud, your identity provider. Everything we build is assigned to you, and client code is never used to train models.
Do you only work with private equity and regulated industries like healthcare, insurance, and lending?
That’s where we go deepest: PE portfolio companies and products where compliance gates every release. It’s also where measured delivery matters most, because the board reads the numbers. But the client list runs wider: logistics, industrial software, fintech, consumer platforms, physical security. If your product has real users and a roadmap behind schedule, the playbook transfers, and the compliance discipline comes free.
How fast can an enterprise AI transformation start?
Two weeks from signature to first standup. We capture your delivery baseline first, so uplift is measured against your own numbers, not industry averages.
What does enterprise AI transformation cost?
Month-to-month, priced per pod, no 12-month contracts. Scale up or down as the roadmap demands. The guarantee is in the contract: no measured uplift, no invoice.
Your AI roadmap is 6 months behind. Your board knows it.
We've closed that gap for 170+ teams over the last decade. Embedded engineers, not consultants. Month-to-month, not 12-month contracts. No measured uplift, no invoice.
