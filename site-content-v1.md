## Decisions locked so far

- Three pages: Home, How we work, Our Work. About is a section on Home.
- Call button everywhere: "Book a 30-minute call. No commitment." No "you'll leave with…" line for now.
- No email address written out on the page. Two actions only: the call button (Calendly) and a "Contact" button that opens an email. Contact appears only in the closing section; the hero has the call button alone.
- Design direction: mockup B v3 (light, dark bands for proof and close), `mockups/b-light-v3.html`. Reviewed against "less is more": every element must earn its place; no decorative motion.
- Visitor-facing step names: Fit call, Job map, Build, Ongoing.
- No autonomy ladder rungs on the site. One stakes line instead.
- No made-up examples (fab shop stays off).
- Three intentions carry the site: Find leaks / Fix how the work gets done / Build for growth. Home states them with one shop proof point each; the shop story on Our Work is organized under the same three.
- The shop story speaks about Wil by name ("Wil found and built…"), not "we," since the work predates Wil Sensing.
- Scheduling saving stays off (realized, but left off by choice).
- Nobody is excluded by wording. Fit is by job, and the copy works for owners, teams at any size of company, and individuals. Avoid "your business," "your people," and "owner" when speaking to the visitor.
- Lead with the "what," not the "how." No "AI where it helps, plain code where it has to be exact" in the hero.
- Never "saved" or "recovered" $217K. ~48% always labeled modeled. Shop never named. No team size. No "streamline."

### Intro
**One job at a time.**

How much it handles on its own depends on the stakes. Some jobs run fully on their own. Others keep a person on every decision. Either way, it's tested on your real work before it's trusted with more.

### The four steps

**1. Fit call**
30 minutes, no commitment. We learn how your work runs and which job is costing you the most, and we both see whether there's a fit.

**2. Job map**
A paid deep look at the job, using your real records. The fee is credited toward the build if you go ahead. [CONFIRM — credit wording; pricing session]

You get:
- **How the job runs today**, and where time and money leak.
- **What to hand off**, ranked, and how much of each part could run on its own.
- **A first-build plan**: what we build, what we need from you, and how we'll measure it.

It comes as a private site you can click through, with a printable version. We can do it over video calls or on site.

**3. Build**
We build what the plan calls for and test it on your real work before it goes live.

**4. Ongoing**
Monthly. We keep testing, hand over more as it proves itself, and take on the next job.

### Is it a fit?
**A good fit if:**
- Someone owns the job and can tell when the work is right.
- We can get at the records and systems the job runs on.
- You want it tested before it's trusted with more.
- It's done by the rules. No scraping, no data misuse, no skipped compliance.

**It helps if:**
- The job comes up again and again.
- It costs something you can see: hours, money, delays, or mistakes.

**Probably not a fit if:**
- The job is all judgment or relationships, with nothing that repeats.
- It's already well automated.
- There's no way to tell if things got better.

### Intro

### Find leaks
**$217K in vendor credits the shop was owed.**

Wil matched 11,139 purchase-order lines against billing and found $217K in vendor credits that weren't coming back. The leak is now stopped going forward. That money was found, not recovered.

### Fix how the work gets done
- **One trusted record.** Customers, cars, orders, calls, and history linked in one place, checked for freshness and tested. Every tool and AI answer works from it, so they act on the shop's real facts instead of guessing.
- **Caller screen.** The front desk sees who's calling, their history, and why they're likely calling before picking up.
- **Morning shop brief.** Every weekday: appointments, parts on hold, estimates waiting, and overdue declined work.

### Build for growth
[PLACEHOLDER — heading becomes "Accelerate growth" and leads with Wil's growth figure]
- **Parts pricing.** Pricing each part instead of a blanket markup. A 90-day model shows about 48% higher gross profit on eligible parts. Modeled, not yet realized.
- **Customer follow-up.** A list of customers who've gone quiet or are due for service, so nobody falls through.
- **Newsletter in the owner's voice.** Tuned from his own edits.

### How it was built to be trusted
- **People approve.** The parts pricing tool only flags. Nothing changes without a person reviewing it.
- **Every claim shows its source.** AI customer summaries link each fact back to where it came from.
- **AI notes are labeled.** Call notes are marked "AI generated" and can be deleted in one click.
- **Customer data stays separate.** Checked across about 100,000 linked records: no customer's data showed up on another customer's record.
- **AI only where it fits.** Some work moved from AI back to plain code where it had to be exact.
