## Part 1: The strategy

### Who it's for
Owners and executives of small and mid-sized businesses. They have tried AI, or they are paying for it, and it hasn't changed much. They don't care how it works. They care about three things: money, hours, and risk.

### The one idea the site has to land
**AI isn't failing you because it's a weak tool. It's failing because it doesn't know your business.** Your information sits in a dozen places, so every AI tool guesses. We fix the foundation first, then build what does the work on top of it.

Everything on the page supports that idea or proves it.

### How the page persuades, in order
1. **Open on the promise.** "Your whole business, finally in one place." Readers should recognize it in the first screen.
2. **Reframe the cause.** "You don't need another tool. You need the ones you have to talk to each other." Half of how the business runs still lives in people's heads, and the rest is spread across systems. That makes the reader feel smart instead of sold to.
3. **Show proof early.** Execs skim for numbers. Put the results second, not at the bottom.
4. **Make the first step safe.** A free 30-minute call, and a Job Map they keep whether or not they buy. Low risk, real value.
5. **Remove the fear of losing control.** The "it starts by drafting, and earns its way up" model is the biggest trust builder. Give it its own section.
6. **Put a person behind it.** Wil maps it, builds it, and runs it. No handoffs, no account manager.
7. **Repeat one call to action.** "Book a free 30-minute call." Same wording every time.

### Words to use, words to avoid
| Avoid | Say instead |
|---|---|
| Semantic layer / context layer | One reliable foundation; one place your AI pulls the truth from |
| Agents, LLMs, fine-tuning | AI, automations, systems |
| Job map (unexplained) | Job Map: a written plan of what we'll fix, what it's worth, and when |
| The ladder / rungs | Starts by drafting, earns its way to running on its own |
| Actionable data | Information your team and your tools can actually use |
| Leakage | Money slipping through the cracks (keep "leakage" only in the proof stat) |

### Tone
Plain, direct, specific. Short sentences. Numbers over adjectives. Never promise "transform your business." Promise measured results with dates.

### 2. The problem

**Heading:**
> ## You don't need another tool. You need the ones you have to talk to each other.

**Copy:**
> Half of how your business runs lives in your best employees' heads. The rest is spread across one system for customers, another for pricing, and a third for the schedule. So your team re-keys the same data, chases missing info, and fixes problems after they've already cost you. And any AI you point at that mess can only guess.

**Short list of pains (scannable):**
- Hours lost every week to copy-paste and double entry
- Money slipping through the cracks: unbilled work, underpriced jobs, missed follow-ups
- Work that stops when one key person is out

---

### 3. What we do

**Heading:**
> ## We fix the foundation, then build what runs on it.

**Copy:**
> **1. One reliable foundation.** We connect your tools, your data, and your customer records so there's one trusted source of truth, with clear privacy and consent rules on who and what can see it.
>
> **2. Systems that do the work.** On top of that foundation, we build the automations your business actually needs, so your team stops reacting and starts getting ahead of problems.
>
> **3. Bring outside services in-house.** Paying monthly for a booking engine or similar tool? We can build one you own, fitted to how you work.
>
> **4. Keep what your experts know.** When your best person goes on vacation, does their work stop? We capture how they do the job and turn it into a reliable, shared system, so specialist knowledge becomes company knowledge.

*Note: item 4 is listed in the plan as an "other idea." It's a strong, relatable hook for owners. Keep it here or promote it to its own section if it tests well.*

### 5. How it works

**Heading:**
> ## Light and fast. No 6-month program. No 200-page deck.

**Step 1. Free discovery call (30 minutes)**
> We learn your business: your goals, how work gets done today, where it gets stuck, and what tools you use. No pitch deck, no obligation.

**Step 2. Your Job Map**
> We walk through your work step by step: How many hours a week does this take? Who else touches it? What breaks? Then we hand you a written plan of every job worth fixing: what we'll build, the measurable improvement, and when you'll see it.
>
> **You keep the Job Map whether or not you hire us.**

**Step 3. The build**
> Priced and timed to the jobs in your map. We work in the order that pays off fastest, and run several jobs at once where it makes sense. Along the way we:
> - Retire tools and steps you no longer need
> - Use simple, exact automation for anything that has to be right every time: matching, math, checks, records
> - Use AI only where it earns its place: messy input like emails, call notes, and photos

**Step 4. A living list**
> Some opportunities only show up once the work starts. New ones go on your list, ranked and ready when you are.

### 6. What's in your Job Map

**Heading:**
> ## A plan you can hold us to.

**Intro:**
> "AI will make us more efficient" isn't a goal. Every job in your map gets a sentence like this:
>
> **"Improve [what we measure] from [where it is today] to [target] by [date]."**

**What you get:**
- **Every job we found,** mapped: the steps, the exceptions, where the data comes from, and who owns it
- **Today's numbers,** recorded so you can check our results against them
- **Where the freed-up time goes,** so saved hours turn into real value, not idle time
- **Who does what:** what the system handles, what a person still handles, and where approval sits
- **How much independence each job gets,** and how far it can safely go
- **The order:** ranked by value, effort, and what depends on what
- **Scope, price, and timeline,** with the dates each result gets checked
- **Honest labels:** if a job can't be measured yet, we say so. And this is a starting list, not a final one.

### 7. You stay in control

**Heading:**
> ## Nothing runs on its own until it's earned it.

**Copy:**
> Every automation starts small and earns more responsibility as it proves itself on real work:
>
> 1. **It drafts.** A person reviews and sends.
> 2. **It acts with your approval.** Nothing happens without a yes.
> 3. **It acts and reports back.** You see everything it did.
> 4. **It runs on its own.** Anything unusual goes to a person.
>
> Most jobs start at step one or two. Your Job Map says how far each one can safely go.

**Built to be trusted (short list):**
- Tested on real work before it's trusted
- Runs on its own schedule, not on someone's laptop
- Has its own account with only the access the job needs. It never pretends to be a person.
- Keeps a log of everything it does
- Has one named owner at your business
- Follows your privacy and consent rules

### 9. Questions owners ask (FAQ)

**Do we have to replace our current tools?**
> No. We make the tools you already have work better. We only retire what you no longer need, and only bring a service in-house when owning it is cheaper or better.

**Is our data safe?**
> Every automation gets the least access its job needs, its own account, a full log, and a named owner on your team. Privacy and consent rules are built into the foundation, not added later.

**What if the AI makes a mistake?**
> Anything that needs to be exact runs on plain, predictable automation, not AI. AI handles messy input only, and new automations start by drafting or asking for approval.

**How long does it take?**
> We work light and fast. Your Job Map gives a timeline for each job, and several jobs can run at once.

**What does it cost?**
> The discovery call and Job Map conversation are free. The build is priced to the specific jobs in your map, so you see the price before you commit. `[TBD: pricing model]`

**What if we don't move forward after the Job Map?**
> You keep it. It's yours either way.

## Part 3: Gaps to fill before launch

These are holes in the source plan. The copy works around them, but each one should be resolved.

1. **Company name.** The plan says "we" and "Wil." The site needs a brand name for the header and page title.
2. **Pricing model.** The plan marks it TBD. The FAQ answer is safe for now, but buyers will ask.
3. **Proof count.** The plan says "four real examples" but lists five results. Confirm which are separate clients.
4. **Proof details.** Add a timeframe to "$200K" (per year? total?) and, if clients allow it, an industry or name for each result. "An auto repair shop" beats "one business."
5. **Testimonials.** Even one quote from the auto repair shop owner would strengthen the results and bio sections.
6. **Privacy and consent specifics.** The plan mentions them but doesn't define them. Execs in regulated industries will want one more sentence.
7. **Wil's photo and booking link.** A face and a working calendar link next to the call to action raise conversions.
